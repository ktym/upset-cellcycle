# Checkpoint inhibition of the APC/C in HeLa cells is mediated by a complex of BUBR1, BUB3, CDC20, and MAD2

- **PMID**: 11535616
- **Authors**: Valery Sudakin, Gordon K. T. Chan, Tim J. Yen
- **Journal**: J Cell Biol (2001) 154:925-936
- **DOI**: 10.1083/jcb.200102093
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11535616/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-046` (`BUB1B|BUB3|MAD2L1` → `CDC20`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The mitotic checkpoint inhibits the ubiquitin ligase activity of the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C) in cells containing unaligned chromosomes, thereby preventing premature mitotic exit. In this study, a physiological APC/C inhibitory factor was purified from HeLa cell extracts and discovered to be a tetrameric complex composed of hBUBR1 (BUB1B), hBUB3 (BUB3), CDC20, and MAD2 (MAD2L1) in near-equimolar stoichiometry, which was named the "Mitotic Checkpoint Complex (MCC)." The inhibitory activity of purified MCC toward mitotic APC/C was more than 3,000-fold greater than that of recombinant MAD2 alone, and most of the APC/C in mitotic cells was shown to be bound and inactivated by the MCC.
