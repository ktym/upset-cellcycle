# 14-3-3 sigma is a p53-regulated inhibitor of G2/M progression

- **PMID**: 9659898
- **Authors**: Heiko Hermeking, Christoph Lengauer, Kornelia Polyak, Tze-Chin He, Lu Zhang, Victor E. Velculescu, Samir Thiagalingam, Kenneth W. Kinzler, Bert Vogelstein
- **Journal**: Mol Cell (1997) 1:3-11
- **DOI**: 10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80002-7
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9659898/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-041` (`TP53` → `SFN|GADD45A`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Exposure of colorectal cancer cells to ionizing radiation induces cell cycle arrest in G1 and G2; while G1 arrest is due to p53-mediated induction of p21, the molecular mechanism of p53-dependent G2 arrest remained unclear. Through quantitative analysis of gene expression profiles (SAGE), this study discovered that 14-3-3 sigma (SFN; Stratifin) is strongly transcriptionally induced by gamma-irradiation and other DNA-damaging agents. A p53-responsive element is located 1.8 kb upstream of the 14-3-3 sigma transcription start site through which p53 directly activates its transcription, and exogenous expression of 14-3-3 sigma in cycling cells induces a G2 arrest. Thus, transcriptional induction of 14-3-3 sigma (SFN) by p53 plays a central role in inhibiting G2/M progression.
