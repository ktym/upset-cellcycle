- **PMID**: 9278511

- **Journal**: Science (1997) 277:1497-1501

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In response to DNA damage, mammalian cells modulate key cell-cycle regulators to prevent mitotic entry. This study isolated the human homolog (CHEK1) of the fission yeast DNA damage checkpoint kinase Chk1 and demonstrated that human Chk1 is modified and activated in response to DNA damage. In vitro, human Chk1 specifically phosphorylates the dual-specificity phosphatase Cdc25C on the conserved serine residue at position 216 (Ser216 / S216), and this phosphorylation promotes the binding of 14-3-3 proteins to Cdc25C, thereby inhibiting the ability of Cdc25C to activate Cdc2 (CDK1). This elucidated a conserved pathway directly linking DNA damage signaling to CDK1 regulation through Chk1-mediated phosphorylation of Cdc25C (S216).
