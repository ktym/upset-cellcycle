## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

D-type cyclins (cyclins D1, D2, and D3) are central cell-cycle regulators that activate CDK4 and CDK6 to drive progression from G1 to S phase, yet the bona fide E3 ubiquitin ligase responsible for their degradation via the ubiquitin–proteasome system had long been debated. This study revealed that AMBRA1 (DCAF3), a substrate receptor of the CUL4–DDB1–RBX1 (CRL4) ubiquitin ligase complex, is the master regulator that directly binds all three D-type cyclins phosphorylated at their C-terminal threonine residue (T286 in cyclin D1) and targets them for polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. Loss of AMBRA1 stabilizes D-type cyclins and causes RB hyperphosphorylation to promote cell proliferation, while reducing the sensitivity of cancer cells to CDK4/6 inhibitors (such as palbociclib and abemaciclib).
