# Acm1 is a negative regulator of the Cdh1-dependent anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome in budding yeast

- **PMID**: 17030612
- **Authors**: Juan S. Martinez, Dah-Eun Jeong, Eunyoung Choi, Brian M. Billings, Mark C. Hall
- **Journal**: Molecular and Cellular Biology (2006) 26:9162-9176
- **DOI**: 10.1128/MCB.00603-06
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17030612/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N20` (`ACM1|BMH1|BMH2` → `CDH1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

This study identifies a novel protein in budding yeast, Acm1 (APC/C-Cdh1 modulator 1), which binds to Cdh1, an activator of the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C), and negatively regulates APC/C-Cdh1 activity. Acm1 forms a cell cycle-regulated complex with Cdh1 together with the 14-3-3 proteins Bmh1 and Bmh2, and Acm1 is required for the association of Cdh1 with Bmh1/Bmh2. Acm1 protein exhibits a cell cycle-regulated expression pattern—appearing in late G1 and disappearing in late M phase—and competitively blocks substrate binding (such as Clb2 and Hsl1) to Cdh1, thereby inhibiting APC/C-Cdh1-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of cyclins and other targets.
