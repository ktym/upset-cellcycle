- **Journal**: Cell (1996) 86:553-564
- **DOI**: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)80129-4

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Full activation of cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) requires both cyclin binding and phosphorylation of a conserved threonine residue in the activation loop (Thr-161 in human Cdc2; Thr-169 in budding yeast Cdc28), a reaction catalyzed by the CDK-activating kinase (CAK). In this study, CAK activity was biochemically purified from budding yeast, and the responsible gene, CAK1, was cloned and identified. Unlike the vertebrate Cdk7-Cyclin H-MAT1 complex, budding yeast Cak1p functions as a monomer and is not a component of the general transcription factor TFIIH. CAK1 is an essential gene, and in the temperature-sensitive cak1-22 mutant, Thr-169 phosphorylation and kinase activity of Cdc28p are dramatically reduced, causing cell-cycle arrest and proving that Cak1p is the physiological CAK for Cdc28 in budding yeast.
