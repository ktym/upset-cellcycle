# Evolutionarily conserved multisubunit RBL2/p130 and E2F4 protein complex represses human cell cycle-dependent genes in quiescence

- **PMID**: 17531812

- **Journal**: Molecular Cell (2007) 26:539-551
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.molcel.2007.04.015
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17531812/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N02` (`RBL2|E2F4|TFDP1` → `LIN52|LIN9|LIN54|LIN37|RBBP4`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

When mammalian cells enter quiescence (G0 phase), the pocket protein family member RBL2/p130 and the transcription factor E2F4-DP1 play a central role in repressing the transcription of cell cycle-related genes. Using proteomics and genome-wide ChIP-chip analyses, this study identified for the first time that in quiescent human cells, p130, E2F4, and DP1 associate with five subunits evolutionarily conserved with the C. elegans and Drosophila MuvB complex (LIN9, LIN37, LIN52, LIN54, and RBBP4) to form a large multisubunit complex termed DREAM (DP, RB-like, E2F, and MuvB). The DREAM complex binds to the promoters of over 800 cell cycle-dependent genes during quiescence to coordinately repress their transcription, and upon cell-cycle entry, p130 dissociates from the MuvB core to relieve repression of these genes.
