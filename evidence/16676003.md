# Phosphorylation of Hsl1 by Hog1 leads to a G2 arrest essential for cell survival at high osmolarity

- **PMID**: 16676003
- **Authors**: Josep Clotet, Xavier Escoté, Miquel Àngel Adrover, Gilad Yaakov, Eloi Garí, Martí Aldea, Eulàlia de Nadal, Francesc Posas
- **Journal**: The EMBO Journal (2006) 25:2338-2346
- **DOI**: 10.1038/sj.emboj.7601095
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16676003/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N28` (`HOG1` → `HSL1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Cell survival under hyperosmotic stress requires regulation of cell-cycle progression by the stress-activated MAP kinase (SAPK) Hog1. This study reveals that in addition to regulating Sic1 in G1 phase, Hog1 also directly controls G2 progression. Activated Hog1 interacts with the checkpoint kinase Hsl1 and directly phosphorylates a residue within the Hsl7-docking site of Hsl1. This causes delocalization of Hsl7 from the septin ring, inhibiting Swe1 degradation and leading to Swe1 accumulation, which induces a G2 arrest essential for cell survival at high osmolarity.
