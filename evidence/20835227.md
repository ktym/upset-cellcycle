# Checkpoint-dependent inhibition of DNA replication initiation by Sld3 and Dbf4 phosphorylation

- **PMID**: 20835227
- **Authors**: Philip Zegerman, John F. X. Diffley
- **Journal**: Nature (2010) 467:474-478
- **DOI**: 10.1038/nature09373
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20835227/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-026` (`RAD53` → `SLD3|DBF4`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

When DNA damage or replication fork stalling occurs during S phase, the S-phase checkpoint is activated and firing of late replication origins is suppressed. This study elucidates the molecular mechanism by which the budding yeast checkpoint effector kinase Rad53 halts late origin firing by inhibiting in parallel two kinase pathways essential for replication initiation (the CDK and DDK pathways). Specifically, Rad53 phosphorylates the downstream CDK substrate Sld3 at multiple sites to inhibit its interactions with Dpb11 and Cdc45, while simultaneously directly phosphorylating the DDK regulatory subunit Dbf4 to suppress DDK (Cdc7-Dbf4) activity. Because simultaneous alanine mutation of the Rad53 phosphorylation sites on Sld3 and Dbf4 allows late origins to fire even under checkpoint activation, these two factors are proven to be the primary targets for checkpoint-mediated inhibition of origin firing in S phase.
