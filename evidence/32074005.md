# Spindle pole bodies function as signal amplifiers in the Mitotic Exit Network

- **PMID**: 32074005
- **Authors**: Ian W. Campbell, Xiaoxue Zhou, Angelika Amon
- **Journal**: Molecular Biology of the Cell (2020) 31:906-916
- **DOI**: 10.1091/mbc.E19-10-0584
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32074005/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N38` (`LTE1` → `KIN4`), `SCE-CC-N40` (`CDC15` → `NUD1`), `SCE-CC-N41` (`NUD1` → `DBF2|MOB1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The Mitotic Exit Network (MEN) in budding yeast is a Ras-like signal transduction cascade that translates nuclear position into a signal to exit from mitosis. This study (PMC7185974) quantitatively analyzed how scaffolding MEN components onto spindle pole bodies (SPBs, the yeast centrosome equivalent) couples the final stages of mitosis to spindle position. Through a series of steps—including Lte1-mediated exclusion of Kin4 from the daughter-bound SPB (dSPB) in the bud, phosphorylation of the SPB scaffold Nud1 by Cdc15, and subsequent recruitment and activation of Dbf2–Mob1—the SPBs function not merely as an on/off switch, but as signal amplifiers.
