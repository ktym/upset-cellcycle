# Budding yeast Cdc20: a target of the spindle checkpoint

- **PMID**: 9461437
- **Authors**: Hwang LH, Lau LF, Smith DL, Mistrot CA, Hardwick KG, Hwang ES, Amon A, Murray AW
- **Journal**: Science (1998) 279:1041-1044
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.279.5353.1041
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9461437/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-034` (`MAD2` → `CDC20`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) regulates the cell cycle by preventing cells with spindle defects from exiting mitosis. In this study, two-hybrid and co-immunoprecipitation analyses revealed that the checkpoint components Mad1, Mad2, and Mad3 physically bind to Cdc20, an activator of the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C). Binding of Mad2 to Cdc20 depended on Mad1, and binding of Mad3 depended on both Mad1 and Mad2. Furthermore, overexpression of Cdc20 overrode M-phase arrest induced by spindle-depolymerizing agents, and mutant Cdc20 resistant to the spindle checkpoint lost the ability to bind Mad proteins, proving that Cdc20 is the direct target of the spindle checkpoint.
