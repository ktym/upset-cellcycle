# cdc25 is a specific tyrosine phosphatase that directly activates p34cdc2

- **PMID**: 1913817
- **Authors**: Jean Gautier, Mark J. Solomon, Robert N. Booher, J. Fernando Bazan, Marc W. Kirschner
- **Journal**: Cell (1991) 67:197-211
- **DOI**: 10.1016/0092-8674(91)90583-k
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1913817/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-035` (`CDC25C|CDC25B` → `CDK1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The M-phase promoting factor (MPF), the cyclin-p34cdc2 (CDK1) complex, is kept in an inactive state during G2 phase by inhibitory phosphorylation of a tyrosine residue (Tyr15) and a threonine residue (Thr14) on the p34cdc2 subunit. This study proved that the cdc25 protein shares significant sequence homology with protein phosphatases and is itself an extraordinarily substrate-specific protein phosphatase. Bacterially expressed and purified cdc25 protein directly dephosphorylated Tyr15 of p34cdc2 and activated it in a minimal reaction system devoid of other eukaryotic cellular components, and induced M-phase entry in Xenopus oocytes and egg extracts. Furthermore, point mutations in the catalytic site abolished both in vitro dephosphorylation activity and in vivo M-phase-inducing ability, establishing that cdc25 is the enzyme that directly dephosphorylates p34cdc2 to activate MPF.
