## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Originally characterized as an autophagy regulator, AMBRA1 had also been implicated in suppressing cell proliferation and tumorigenesis, but its direct molecular mechanism in the cell cycle remained unknown. This study demonstrated that AMBRA1 functions as a substrate adaptor for the Cullin 4 (CRL4) ubiquitin ligase complex to promote the ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of phosphorylated D-type cyclins (particularly cyclin D1), thereby regulating the G1/S transition. Loss of AMBRA1 leads to abnormal accumulation of cyclin D and elevated RB phosphorylation, causing premature S-phase entry, DNA replication stress, and genomic instability. These findings establish that CRL4–AMBRA1-mediated degradation of cyclin D is essential for proper timing of S-phase entry and maintenance of genomic integrity.
