# Downregulation of PP2A(Cdc55) phosphatase by separase initiates mitotic exit in budding yeast

- **PMID**: 16713564
- **Authors**: Queralt E, Lehane C, Novak B, Uhlmann F
- **Journal**: Cell (2006) 125:719-732
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.cell.2006.03.038
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16713564/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-059` (`ESP1` → `CDC55`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Mitotic exit in budding yeast is initiated by Cdk-dependent phosphorylation of the nucleolar inhibitor Net1 and the consequent release of the phosphatase Cdc14 from the nucleolus. However, it remained unclear why Net1 phosphorylation and Cdc14 release do not occur during metaphase, when Cdk activity is already high, but are triggered specifically at anaphase onset. This study discovered that the protein phosphatase PP2A(Cdc55) keeps Net1 underphosphorylated during metaphase to prevent premature Cdc14 release. Furthermore, at anaphase onset, separase (Esp1), which triggers sister chromatid separation, downregulates PP2A(Cdc55) phosphatase activity independently of its protease activity, thereby enabling Cdk-mediated Net1 phosphorylation and Cdc14 release from the nucleolus.
