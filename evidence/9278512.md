# Mitotic and G2 checkpoint control: regulation of 14-3-3 protein binding by phosphorylation of Cdc25C on serine-216

- **PMID**: 9278512
- **Authors**: Cheng-Yuan Peng, Paul R. Graves, Richard S. Thoma, Zhiqi Wu, Andrey S. Shaw, Helen Piwnica-Worms
- **Journal**: Science (1997) 277:1501-1505
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.277.5331.1501
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9278512/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-039` (`CHEK1|CHEK2` → `CDC25C`), `HSA-CC-040` (`YWHAE` → `CDC25C`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Human Cdc25C is a dual-specificity phosphatase that dephosphorylates Cdc2 (CDK1) to trigger mitotic entry. In this study, Cdc25C was shown to be phosphorylated on serine residue 216 (Ser216 / S216) during interphase and upon DNA damage, resulting in specific binding to 14-3-3 family proteins (such as YWHAE) and negative regulation of Cdc25C. The checkpoint kinase Chk1 (CHEK1) directly phosphorylates Cdc25C on Ser216 to create a 14-3-3 binding site, and overexpression of a Cdc25C mutant (S216A) in which Ser216 is substituted with alanine—abolishing phosphorylation and 14-3-3 binding—perturbs normal mitotic timing and uncouples the G2 DNA damage checkpoint arrest.
