# Glycogen synthase kinase-3beta regulates cyclin D1 proteolysis and subcellular localization

- **PMID**: 9832503
- **Authors**: J. A. Diehl, M. Cheng, M. F. Roussel, C. J. Sherr
- **Journal**: Genes & Development (1998) 12:3499-3511
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.12.22.3499

- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-006` (`GSK3B` → `CCND1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Cyclin D1 is an unstable protein whose proteasomal degradation is positively regulated by phosphorylation on a single threonine residue near its C-terminus (Thr-286). This study demonstrated that glycogen synthase kinase-3beta (GSK-3beta) specifically phosphorylates cyclin D1 on Thr-286, thereby triggering rapid cyclin D1 turnover. Furthermore, GSK-3beta enters the nucleus during S phase to phosphorylate cyclin D1, directly linking the nuclear-to-cytoplasmic redistribution (nuclear export) of cyclin D1 with its proteolytic degradation.
