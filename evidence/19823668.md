# Dual regulation by pairs of cyclin-dependent protein kinases and histone deacetylases controls G1 transcription in budding yeast

- **PMID**: 19823668
- **Authors**: Dongqing Huang, Supipi Kaluarachchi, Dewald van Dyk, Helena Friesen, Richelle Sopko, Wei Ye, Nazareth Bastajian, Jason Moffat, Holly Sassi, Michael Costanzo, Brenda J. Andrews

- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-004` (`PHO85|PCL9` → `WHI5`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Through a comprehensive synthetic genetic array (SGA) analysis using CLN3 as a query, a second CDK pathway and histone deacetylases (HDACs) acting in concert with the Cln3-Cdc28 kinase in Start transcriptional regulation during G1 phase were identified. G1/S transcriptional repression by Whi5 involves its association with HDACs (Hos3 and Rpd3 complexes), establishing a chromatin modification-mediated repression mechanism analogous to mammalian Rb. Meanwhile, the G1-specific cyclin-CDK complex Pcl9-Pho85 localizes to SBF/MBF target promoters and functions as a physiological kinase that physically binds and directly phosphorylates Whi5. Dual phosphorylation regulation by Cln3-Cdc28 (which promotes dissociation of Whi5 from SBF) and Pcl9-Pho85 (which blocks association of Whi5 with HDACs) ensures robust relief of Whi5 repressive activity and initiation of Start transcription.
