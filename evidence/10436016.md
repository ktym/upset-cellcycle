# The spindle checkpoint of budding yeast depends on a tight complex between the Mad1 and Mad2 proteins

- **PMID**: 10436016
- **Authors**: Chen RH, Brady DM, Smith D, Murray AW, Hardwick KG
- **Journal**: Mol Biol Cell (1999) 10:2607-2618
- **DOI**: 10.1091/mbc.10.8.2607
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10436016/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-035` (`MAD1` → `MAD2`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) is a mechanism that arrests the cell cycle at metaphase when defects in kinetochore–spindle microtubule attachment or spindle abnormalities are present. This study cloned the budding yeast MAD2 gene and identified Mad2p, a 196-amino-acid protein present at constant levels throughout the cell cycle. Gel filtration and co-immunoprecipitation analyses showed that Mad2p forms a tight complex with another checkpoint component, Mad1p, independently of cell cycle stage or the presence of other checkpoint factors. Furthermore, deletion and mutational analyses revealed that the association between Mad1p and Mad2p is essential for Mad1p hyperphosphorylation and spindle checkpoint function.
