# The human Myt1 kinase preferentially phosphorylates Cdc2 on threonine 14 and localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi complex

- **PMID**: 9001210
- **Authors**: Feng Liu, John J. Stanton, Zhiqi Wu, Helen Piwnica-Worms
- **Journal**: Mol Cell Biol (1997) 17:571-583
- **DOI**: 10.1128/MCB.17.2.571
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9001210/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-034` (`PKMYT1` → `CDK1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Mitotic entry is triggered by the activation of the Cdc2 (CDK1)/cyclin B complex, whose full activation requires dephosphorylation of Thr14 and Tyr15. In this study, the human Myt1 (PKMYT1) kinase belonging to the Wee1 family was cloned and its biochemical properties and subcellular localization were analyzed. Recombinant human Myt1 phosphorylates cyclin B-bound Cdc2 on both Thr14 and Tyr15 to inhibit Cdc2 activity, exhibiting a strong substrate preference for Thr14 (T14). Furthermore, unlike nuclear human Wee1, human Myt1 associates with intracellular membranes of the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi complex via a C-terminal hydrophobic region, negatively regulating cytoplasmic Cdc2-cyclin B complexes.
