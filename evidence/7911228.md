# The p21 inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinases controls DNA replication by interaction with PCNA

- **PMID**: 7911228
- **Authors**: Shou Waga, Gregory J. Hannon, David Beach, Bruce Stillman
- **Journal**: Nature (1994) 369:574-578
- **DOI**: 10.1038/369574a0

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Transcriptionally induced by the tumor suppressor protein p53, p21 (CDKN1A) exists in normal human cells in quaternary complexes with cyclins, CDKs, and proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA). Using an in vitro reconstituted SV40 DNA replication system, this study revealed that p21 directly inhibits PCNA-dependent DNA replication even in the absence of cyclin/CDK complexes. Furthermore, it demonstrated that by binding directly to PCNA, p21 blocks the ability of PCNA to activate DNA polymerase delta, the principal replicative polymerase. Through this mechanism, the interaction between p21 and PCNA plays a crucial role in regulating cell cycle progression and coordinating DNA replication and repair during p53-mediated growth suppression.
