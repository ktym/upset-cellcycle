# Isolation of a human cyclin cDNA: evidence for cyclin mRNA and protein regulation in the cell cycle and for interaction with p34cdc2

- **PMID**: 2570636
- **Authors**: Jonathan Pines, Tony Hunter
- **Journal**: Cell (1989) 58:833-846
- **DOI**: 10.1016/0092-8674(89)90936-7
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2570636/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-032` (`CCNB1` → `CDK1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

This landmark study isolated the cDNA for human B-type cyclin (cyclin B1; CCNB1) and determined its nucleotide and predicted amino acid sequences for the first time. It showed that human cyclin B1 mRNA levels are regulated throughout the cell cycle, increasing in G2 phase to approximately four times the level in G1 phase, while protein levels steadily accumulate during G2 phase to at least 20 times their G1 level before being abruptly degraded at mitosis. Furthermore, it demonstrated that in G2/M phase, cyclin B1 forms a complex with p34cdc2 (CDK1), the human homolog of fission yeast cdc2+, and that this complex possesses histone H1 kinase activity (activity as the M-phase promoting factor, MPF).
