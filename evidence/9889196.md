# Phosphorylation of mammalian CDC6 by cyclin A/CDK2 regulates its subcellular localization

- **PMID**: 9889196
- **Authors**: B. O. Petersen, J. Lukas, C. S. Sørensen, J. Bartek, K. Helin
- **Journal**: The EMBO Journal (1999) 18:396-410
- **DOI**: 10.1093/emboj/18.2.396
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9889196/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-024` (`CCNA2|CDK2` → `CDC6`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Mammalian CDC6, an essential regulator of DNA replication initiation, localizes to the nucleus during G1 phase but relocalizes to the cytoplasm upon entry into S phase. This study demonstrated that CDC6 specifically interacts with the active cyclin A/CDK2 complex (CCNA2|CDK2) via an N-terminal Cy motif and is phosphorylated at three N-terminal CDK consensus sites. Phosphorylation of CDC6 by cyclin A/CDK2 triggers rapid relocalization of CDC6 from the nucleus to the cytoplasm, functioning as a negative regulatory mechanism to prevent DNA re-replication during S and G2 phases.
