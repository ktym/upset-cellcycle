# Reversal of DDK-Mediated MCM Phosphorylation by Rif1-PP1 Regulates Replication Initiation and Replisome Stability Independently of ATR/Chk1

- **PMID**: 28273499
- **Authors**: Robert C. Alver, Gaganmeet Singh Chadha, Peter J. Gillespie, J. Julian Blow
- **Journal**: Cell Reports (2017) 18:2508-2520
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.celrep.2017.02.042
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28273499/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N10` (`RIF1|PPP1CA` → `MCM4`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Initiation of DNA replication requires phosphorylation of the MCM2–7 helicase complex by Dbf4-dependent kinase (DDK), and hyper-phosphorylation of chromatin-bound MCM4 strongly correlates with replication onset. Using *Xenopus laevis* egg extracts and human cells, this study revealed that protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) recruited to chromatin by RIF1 reverses DDK-dependent phosphorylation of MCM (specifically MCM4). Loss of RIF1 increases MCM phosphorylation levels and the rate of replication origin initiation (firing), while compromising the ability of cells to restrain origin firing when challenged with replication inhibitors. Thus, the balance between DDK-mediated phosphorylation and RIF1–PP1-mediated dephosphorylation governs the phosphorylation state of MCM to regulate replication initiation and replisome stability.
