# Dbf2-Mob1 drives relocalization of protein phosphatase Cdc14 to the cytoplasm during exit from mitosis

- **PMID**: 19221193
- **Authors**: Dane A. Mohl, Michael J. Huddleston, Therese S. Collingwood, Roland S. Annan, Raymond J. Deshaies
- **Journal**: Journal of Cell Biology (2009) 184:527-540
- **DOI**: 10.1083/jcb.200812022
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19221193/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N42` (`DBF2|MOB1` → `CDC14`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Mitotic exit in budding yeast is driven by the protein phosphatase Cdc14, which antagonizes Cdk activity. Cdc14 is sequestered in the nucleolus throughout interphase, but upon activation of the mitotic exit network (MEN) in anaphase, it disperses throughout the cell (into the cytoplasm). This study reveals that the terminal MEN kinase complex Dbf2–Mob1 directly phosphorylates serine and threonine residues adjacent to the C-terminal nuclear localization signal (NLS) of Cdc14, inhibiting NLS activity and retaining Cdc14 in the cytoplasm to promote dephosphorylation of cytoplasmic substrates, mitotic exit, and cytokinesis.
