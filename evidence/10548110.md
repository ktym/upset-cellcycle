# Accumulation of cyclin B1 requires E2F and cyclin-A-dependent rearrangement of the anaphase-promoting complex

- **PMID**: 10548110

- **Journal**: Nature (1999) 401:815-818
- **DOI**: 10.1038/44611
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10548110/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-026` (`CCNA2|CDK2` → `FZR1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In the mammalian somatic cell cycle, continuous transcription of E2F target genes is required to coordinate S-phase progression with cell division after passage through the restriction point (R point). Using a real-time in vivo protein stability assay, this study revealed that repression of E2F activity or inhibition of cyclin A-dependent kinase (Cyclin A–CDK2) in S phase triggers re-association of the ubiquitin ligase complex APC with its activating subunit Cdh1 (FZR1), inducing premature destruction of cyclin B1. Furthermore, expression of a phosphorylation-deficient Cdh1 mutant or immunodepletion of cyclin A resulted in the assembly of active Cdh1–APC complexes even in S-phase cells. Thus, E2F-dependent, Cyclin A/Cdk2-mediated phosphorylation of Cdh1 to dissociate and inactivate it from the APC is essential for timely accumulation of cyclin B1 from S to G2 phase and proper cell cycle progression.
