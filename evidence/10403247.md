# Sister-chromatid separation at anaphase onset is promoted by cleavage of the cohesin subunit Scc1

- **PMID**: 10403247
- **Authors**: Uhlmann F, Lottspeich F, Nasmyth K
- **Journal**: Nature (1999) 400:37-42
- **DOI**: 10.1038/21831
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10403247/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-039` (`ESP1` → `MCD1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Cohesion between sister chromatids is established during DNA replication by the cohesin complex and is essential for chromosome alignment during metaphase, but must be dissolved at anaphase onset. In budding yeast, loss of sister-chromatid cohesion depends on a separin (separase) protein called Esp1 and is accompanied by dissociation of the cohesin subunit Scc1 (Mcd1) from chromosomes. This study demonstrated that Esp1 triggers the dissociation of Scc1 from chromosomes by promoting its proteolytic cleavage. Furthermore, a mutant form of Scc1 resistant to Esp1-dependent cleavage blocked both Scc1 dissociation from chromosomes and sister-chromatid separation.
