# The Cdc4/34/53 pathway targets Cdc6p for proteolysis in budding yeast

- **PMID**: 9312054
- **Authors**: Lucy S. Drury, Gareth Perkins, John F. X. Diffley
- **Journal**: The EMBO Journal (1997) 16:5966-5976
- **DOI**: 10.1093/emboj/16.19.5966
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9312054/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-021` (`CDC4|CDC34|CDC53` → `CDC6`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The budding yeast DNA replication initiation factor Cdc6p directs the assembly of the pre-replicative complex (pre-RC) during G1 phase, and its rapid degradation from late G1 through S and M phases helps ensure that DNA replication occurs only once per cell cycle. In this study, destabilization of Cdc6p at the G1/S transition was shown to be caused by proteolysis dependent on the Cdc4, Cdc34, and Cdc53 (the SCF(Cdc4) ubiquitin ligase complex and E2 enzyme Cdc34) pathway. Furthermore, a degradation signal (degron) containing CDK consensus phosphorylation sites was identified within the N-terminal 47 amino acid region of Cdc6p, and a mutant Cdc6p lacking this N-terminal region was demonstrated to be stabilized throughout the cell cycle.
