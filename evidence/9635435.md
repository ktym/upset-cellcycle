# An ESP1/PDS1 complex regulates loss of sister chromatid cohesion at the metaphase to anaphase transition in yeast

- **PMID**: 9635435
- **Authors**: Ciosk R, Zachariae W, Michaelis C, Shevchenko A, Mann M, Nasmyth K
- **Journal**: Cell (1998) 93:1067-1076
- **DOI**: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81211-8
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9635435/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-038` (`PDS1` → `ESP1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Sister chromatid cohesion during G2 and M phases depends on the cohesin subunit Scc1p (Mcd1p), and loss of cohesion at the metaphase-to-anaphase transition requires degradation of Pds1p by the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C). In this study, destruction of Pds1p was shown to be the sole role of the APC in triggering the dissociation of Scc1p from chromatids. Furthermore, Pds1p was found to form a stable complex with Esp1p (separase), a 180 kDa protein essential for Scc1p dissociation from sister chromatids and sister chromatid separation. Based on these findings, a model was proposed in which Pds1p inhibits Esp1p through direct binding, and APC-mediated degradation of Pds1p liberates Esp1p to promote sister chromatid separation.
