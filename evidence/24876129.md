# Cyclin D activates the Rb tumor suppressor by mono-phosphorylation

- **PMID**: 24876129
- **Authors**: Anil M. Narasimha, Manuel Kaulich, Gary S. Shapiro, Yoon J. Choi, Piotr Sicinski, Steven F. Dowdy
- **Journal**: eLife (2014) 3:e02872
- **DOI**: 10.7554/eLife.02872

- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N03` (`CCND1|CDK4` → `RB1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In the classical model of G1 progression, cyclin D-CDK4/6 was thought to progressively phosphorylate RB at multiple sites (hypo-phosphorylation) in early G1 to partially inactivate it and gradually release E2F transcription factors. Using two-dimensional isoelectric focusing and biochemical analyses, this study reveals that in early G1, RB exists exclusively as a mono-phosphorylated form phosphorylated at only a single site by cyclin D-CDK4/6. Composed of 14 independent isoforms, mono-phosphorylated RB remains functionally active, binding E2F and regulating transcription during early G1 and the DNA damage response. Complete inactivation of RB and release of E2F occur only at the late G1 Restriction Point via quantum hyper-phosphorylation by cyclin E-CDK2.
