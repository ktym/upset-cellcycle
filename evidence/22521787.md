# Phosphoregulation of Spc105 by Mps1 and PP1 regulates Bub1 localization to kinetochores

- **PMID**: 22521787
- **Authors**: Nitobe London, Steven Ceto, Jeffrey A. Ranish, Sue Biggins
- **Journal**: Current Biology (2012) 22:900-906
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.cub.2012.03.052
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22521787/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N29` (`MPS1` → `SPC105`), `SCE-CC-N35` (`GLC7` → `SPC105`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) is a surveillance mechanism that blocks anaphase onset until all sister chromatids establish bioriented microtubule attachments and depends on recruiting checkpoint proteins to kinetochores. This study identifies Mps1 as the major kinase activity copurifying with budding yeast kinetochore particles and demonstrates that the conserved kinetochore protein Spc105 (human KNL1 ortholog) is its direct substrate. Phosphorylation of conserved MELT motifs within Spc105 by Mps1 recruits Bub1 to kinetochores, whereas type I protein phosphatase PP1 (Glc7) reverses this phosphorylation to control SAC silencing.
