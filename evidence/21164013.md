# Greatwall phosphorylates an inhibitor of protein phosphatase 2A that is essential for mitosis

- **PMID**: 21164013
- **Authors**: Satoru Mochida, Sarah L. Maslen, Mark Skehel, Tim Hunt
- **Journal**: Science (2010) 330:1670-1673
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.1195689
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21164013/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N18` (`MASTL` → `ENSA|ARPP19`), `HSA-CC-N19` (`ENSA|ARPP19` → `PPP2R2A`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Entry into and maintenance of mitosis in eukaryotic cells require not only the activation of cyclin B-CDK1, but also the suppression of protein phosphatase 2A-B55 (PP2A-B55) complexes that dephosphorylate CDK1 substrates. This study reveals that the mitotic kinase Greatwall (MASTL in humans) directly phosphorylates a conserved serine residue (Ser67 in ENSA) of the small protein alpha-endosulfine (ENSA) and its paralog ARPP-19. Greatwall-phosphorylated ENSA/ARPP-19 specifically binds to PP2A-B55delta and potently inhibits its phosphatase activity, protecting the phosphorylation state of CDK1 substrates to drive and maintain cells in mitosis.
