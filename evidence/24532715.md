# Rif1 controls DNA replication by directing Protein Phosphatase 1 to reverse Cdc7-mediated phosphorylation of the MCM complex

- **PMID**: 24532715
- **Authors**: Shin-Ichiro Hiraga, Gina M. Alvino, FuJung Chang, Hui-Yong Lian, Akila Sridhar, Takashi Kubota, Bonita J. Brewer, Michael Weinreich, M. K. Raghuraman, Anne D. Donaldson
- **Journal**: Genes & Development (2014) 28:372-383
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.231258.113
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24532715/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N13` (`RIF1|GLC7` → `MCM4`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

This study demonstrates that Rif1, previously known as a telomere-binding protein, controls genome-wide DNA replication timing by recruiting Protein Phosphatase 1 (PP1/Glc7) to replication origins to promote dephosphorylation of the MCM complex. Rif1 binds Glc7 via conserved PP1-binding motifs (RVxF and SILK motifs) in its N-terminal domain and counteracts Dbf4-dependent kinase (DDK: Cdc7-Dbf4)-mediated phosphorylation of Mcm4 to restrain premature origin firing. Deletion of RIF1 increases premature Mcm4 phosphorylation and partially suppresses the temperature sensitivity of a cdc7-1 mutant, showing that the Rif1-Glc7 complex opposes DDK-mediated Mcm4 phosphorylation to regulate replication initiation.
