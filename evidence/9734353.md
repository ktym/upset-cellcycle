# Direct binding of CDC20 protein family members activates the anaphase-promoting complex in mitosis and G1

- **PMID**: 9734353
- **Authors**: Guowei Fang, Hongtao Yu, Marc W. Kirschner
- **Journal**: Mol Cell (1998) 2:163-171
- **DOI**: 10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80126-4
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9734353/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-052` (`FZR1` → `CCNB1|CDC20`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Activation of the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C) is essential for the onset of anaphase and exit from mitosis. This study demonstrated that vertebrate APC is activated during mitosis and G1 by direct binding of hCDC20 (CDC20) and hCDH1 (FZR1), respectively, catalyzing the ubiquitination of substrates such as cyclin B (CCNB1). In HeLa cells, hCDC20 protein levels and its association with the APC peak during mitosis, and hCDC20 itself is degraded upon mitotic exit; from late mitosis into G1, hCDH1 binds to the APC to form APC/C-CDH1, mediating the ubiquitination and degradation of mitotic cyclins and remaining mitotic regulators to maintain the G1 state.
