# Cyclin Cln3 is retained at the ER and released by the J chaperone Ydj1 in late G1 to trigger cell cycle entry

- **PMID**: 17560371
- **Authors**: Emili Vergés, Neus Colomina, Eloi Garí, Carme Gallego, Martí Aldea
- **Journal**: Molecular Cell (2007) 26:649-662
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.molcel.2007.04.023
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17560371/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N04` (`YDJ1|SSA1` → `CLN3`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

This study elucidates the localization control mechanism of the G1 cyclin Cln3, which couples cell growth to cell-cycle entry (Start) in budding yeast. Whereas Cln3 was previously thought to passively accumulate in the nucleus throughout G1 phase, this work shows that in early G1 cells Cln3 is tethered and retained at the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane in a manner dependent on its binding to Cdc28. Furthermore, in late G1, the J-domain chaperone Ydj1, a cochaperone of Hsp70 (such as Ssa1), is essential for the release of Cln3 from the ER and its nuclear accumulation, acting as a rate-limiting factor to trigger cell-cycle entry. Because protein synthesis rates govern chaperone availability, the Ydj1/Ssa1 chaperone system is proposed to function as a sensor that transduces cell growth signals into nuclear translocation of Cln3.
