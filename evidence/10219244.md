# Exit from mitosis is triggered by Tem1-dependent release of the protein phosphatase Cdc14 from nucleolar RENT complex

- **PMID**: 10219244
- **Authors**: Shou W, Seol JH, Shevchenko A, Baskerville C, Moazed D, Chen ZWS, Jang J, Shevchenko A, Charbonneau H, Deshaies RJ
- **Journal**: Cell (1999) 97:233-244
- **DOI**: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)80733-3
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10219244/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-052` (`NET1` → `CDC14`), `SCE-CC-053` (`TEM1` → `CDC14`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Mitotic exit and inactivation of cyclin-dependent kinases (Cdks) in budding yeast require the dual-specificity phosphatase Cdc14 and a signaling network termed the Mitotic Exit Network (MEN). This study revealed that Cdc14 activity is regulated by a nucleolar complex called RENT (regulator of nucleolar silencing and telophase), which contains a novel nucleolar protein, Net1 (Cfi1). From G1 to metaphase, Net1 sequesters Cdc14 within the nucleolar RENT complex and directly inhibits its phosphatase activity. In late anaphase, Cdc14 is released from the nucleolar RENT complex into the nucleoplasm and cytoplasm in a manner dependent on the MEN GTPase Tem1, triggering mitotic exit through dephosphorylation of target substrates.
