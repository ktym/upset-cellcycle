# Mcm2 is a target of regulation by Cdc7-Dbf4 during the initiation of DNA synthesis

- **PMID**: 9407029
- **Authors**: M. Lei, Y. Kawasaki, M. R. Young, M. Kihara, A. Sugino, B. K. Tye
- **Journal**: Genes & Development (1997) 11:3365-3374
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.11.24.3365
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9407029/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-023` (`CDC7|DBF4` → `MCM2`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The hexameric Mcm2-7 complex, essential for eukaryotic DNA replication initiation, undergoes cell-cycle-specific phosphorylation at the onset of S phase. In this study, DBF4, encoding the regulatory subunit of Cdc7 kinase, was isolated from a suppressor screen of the mcm2-1 mutant, and the Cdc7-Dbf4 (DDK) kinase complex was shown to physically interact with Mcm2. Furthermore, Cdc7-Dbf4 directly phosphorylates Mcm2 (as well as Mcm3, Mcm4, and Mcm6) in vitro, and inhibiting Cdc7-Dbf4 activity at the G1/S transition abolishes Mcm2 phosphorylation in vivo, demonstrating that Mcm2 phosphorylation by Cdc7-Dbf4 at the G1/S boundary is a critical step in the initiation of DNA synthesis at replication origins.
