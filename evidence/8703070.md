- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.273.5280.1377

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The phosphatase Cdc25 (CDC25C), which dephosphorylates and activates Cdc2 (CDK1) to control mitotic entry, undergoes a marked increase in its own enzymatic activity at the onset of mitosis through extensive phosphorylation of its N-terminal regulatory region. In this study, the kinase responsible for binding and phosphorylating the N-terminal domain of Cdc25 was purified and molecularly cloned from Xenopus egg extracts, identifying it as Plx1 (corresponding to mammalian PLK1), a homolog of Polo-like kinase. Recombinant Plx1 was demonstrated to directly phosphorylate Cdc25 and stimulate its phosphatase activity in vitro, showing that phosphorylation and activation of CDC25C by PLK1 play a central role in the trigger and amplification circuit of mitotic entry.
