# Temporal and spatial control of cyclin B1 destruction in metaphase

- **PMID**: 10559878
- **Authors**: Paul Clute, Jonathan Pines
- **Journal**: Nat Cell Biol (1999) 1:82-87
- **DOI**: 10.1038/10049
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10559878/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-049` (`CDC20` → `CCNB1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Progression through mitosis is regulated by ubiquitin-dependent proteolysis of key cell cycle regulatory proteins. Using GFP-tagged cyclin B1 (CCNB1), this study quantitatively analyzed the degradation of cyclin B1 in real time in living cells and revealed that cyclin B1 degradation begins in metaphase, immediately after the last chromosome aligns on the metaphase plate and the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) is inactivated. Concomitant with the onset of degradation in metaphase, cyclin B1 disappears from spindle poles and chromosomes, and reactivation of the checkpoint during metaphase with a microtubule poison (taxol) immediately halts cyclin B1 destruction by the APC/C (APC/C-CDC20).
