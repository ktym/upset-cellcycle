# Direct regulation of Treslin by cyclin-dependent kinase is essential for the onset of DNA replication

- **PMID**: 21646402
- **Authors**: Akiko Kumagai, Anna Shevchenko, Andrej Shevchenko, William G. Dunphy
- **Journal**: Journal of Cell Biology (2011) 193:995-1007
- **DOI**: 10.1083/jcb.201102003
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21646402/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N11` (`CCNE1|CDK2` → `TICRR`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Initiation of chromosomal DNA replication in vertebrates requires complex formation between the TopBP1-interacting protein Treslin (TICRR) and TopBP1, as well as recruitment of Cdc45 to replication origins. This study demonstrates that S-phase cyclin-dependent kinase CDK2-cyclin E (and cyclin A) directly phosphorylates conserved consensus sites on Treslin (Ser1000 in humans and Ser976 in Xenopus). This phosphorylation of Treslin by CDK2 is the critical step enabling Treslin to bind the N-terminal BRCT domain region of TopBP1, and Treslin mutants lacking these phosphorylation sites fail to bind TopBP1 and initiate DNA replication.
