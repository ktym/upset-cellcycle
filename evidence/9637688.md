# The checkpoint protein MAD2 and the mitotic regulator CDC20 form a ternary complex with the anaphase-promoting complex to control anaphase initiation

- **PMID**: 9637688
- **Authors**: Guowei Fang, Hongtao Yu, Marc W. Kirschner
- **Journal**: Genes Dev (1998) 12:1871-1883
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.12.12.1871
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9637688/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-045` (`MAD2L1` → `CDC20`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) delays the onset of anaphase until all chromosomes are aligned at the metaphase plate. While anaphase initiation requires activation of the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C) by CDC20, this study revealed that the human checkpoint protein hMAD2 (MAD2L1) directly binds to CDC20 to form a ternary hMAD2-CDC20-APC complex, thereby inhibiting CDC20-mediated activation of the APC. Recombinant hMAD2 exists in two conformational states, a monomer and a tetramer; although both can bind CDC20, only the tetramer (the active conformation) inhibits APC activation and causes mitotic arrest in Xenopus embryos. This established the molecular mechanism by which MAD2 binds CDC20 to block APC/C activation and prevent anaphase onset.
