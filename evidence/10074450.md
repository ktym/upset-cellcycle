# Inhibitory phosphorylation of the APC regulator Hct1 is controlled by the kinase Cdc28 and the phosphatase Cdc14

- **PMID**: 10074450
- **Authors**: Jaspersen SL, Charles JF, Morgan DO
- **Journal**: Curr Biol (1999) 9:227-236
- **DOI**: 10.1016/s0960-9822(99)80111-0
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10074450/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-048` (`CDC28|CLB2|CLB5` → `CDH1`), `SCE-CC-049` (`CDC14` → `CDH1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Mitotic exit requires ubiquitin-dependent degradation of M-phase cyclins by the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C) and its activating subunit Hct1 (Cdh1), accompanied by CDK inactivation. This study showed that Hct1 is phosphorylated in vivo at multiple CDK consensus sites during cell cycle stages when Cdc28 (Cdk1) activity is high and APC activity is low. Purified Cdc28–cyclin complexes phosphorylated Hct1 at these sites in vitro, and phosphorylated Hct1 completely lost its ability to activate the APC. Furthermore, the phosphatase Cdc14, which is essential for APC activation in vivo, directly dephosphorylated Hct1 to reverse Cdc28-mediated inhibition and reactivate APC–Hct1.
