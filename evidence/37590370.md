# In silico protein interaction screening uncovers DONSON's role in replication initiation

- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N13` (`DONSON` → `GINS1|TOPBP1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

A central event in eukaryotic DNA replication initiation is the assembly of the CMG (CDC45–MCM2–7–GINS) helicase through the recruitment of CDC45 and GINS to MCM2–7 double hexamers loaded at replication origins. While a pre-loading complex (pre-LC) containing Sld2 delivers GINS to MCM2–7 in budding yeast, its functional counterpart in vertebrates had long remained elusive. Using AlphaFold-based *in silico* protein–protein interaction screening combined with biochemical and genetic validation, this study discovered that DONSON, a protein mutated in microcephalic primordial dwarfism, is required for CMG assembly in vertebrates. DONSON functions as a scaffold for a vertebrate pre-LC that bundles GINS, TOPBP1, and DNA polymerase ε (Pol ε), docking onto MCM2–7 to deliver GINS to CDC45–MCM2–7 and assemble the CMG helicase. Moreover, a patient-derived DONSON mutation was shown to compromise CMG assembly and recapitulate microcephalic dwarfism in a mouse model.
