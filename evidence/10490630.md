## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The morphogenesis checkpoint in budding yeast senses budding defects caused by perturbations in the actin cytoskeleton or septin ring assembly and induces G2 arrest by stabilizing and accumulating the Cdc28-inhibitory kinase Swe1p. This study revealed that the Nim1-related kinase Hsl1p and the adapter protein Hsl7p act in concert as negative regulators that target Swe1p for degradation. Both Hsl1p and Hsl7p are essential for Swe1p degradation during G2/M in an unperturbed cell cycle; Hsl1p is periodically expressed and promotes Hsl7p phosphorylation, while Hsl7p physically forms a complex with Swe1p to promote its recognition by the ubiquitination and degradation machinery.
