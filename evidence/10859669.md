# Chk1 is an essential kinase that is regulated by Atr and required for the G(2)/M DNA damage checkpoint

- **PMID**: 10859669
- **Authors**: Qinghua Liu, Shantha Guntuku, Xiang-Shuo Cui, Satoshi Matsuoka, David Cortez, Kenji Tamai, Gen Luo, Sandra Carattini-Rivera, Francis DeMayo, Allan Bradley, Lawrence A. Donehower, Stephen J. Elledge
- **Journal**: Genes Dev (2000) 14:1448-1459
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.14.12.1448

- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-030` (`ATR` → `CHEK1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The evolutionarily conserved protein kinase Chk1 (CHEK1) plays an essential role in mammalian cell viability, embryonic development, and cell cycle checkpoint control in response to DNA damage. This study revealed that conditionally CHK1-deficient ES cells exhibit a defective G2/M checkpoint in response to DNA damage. Furthermore, in human cells, Chk1 is phosphorylated on Ser-345 (S345) in response to ultraviolet (UV) irradiation, ionizing radiation (IR), and the replication stress inducer hydroxyurea (HU). Overexpression of wild-type ATR enhanced UV-induced Chk1 S345 phosphorylation whereas overexpression of a kinase-defective ATR mutant inhibited it, demonstrating that ATR acts as an upstream kinase that phosphorylates and regulates Chk1 upon replication stress or UV damage.
