# 14-3-3sigma is required to prevent mitotic catastrophe after DNA damage

- **PMID**: 10524633
- **Authors**: Timothy A. Chan, Heiko Hermeking, Christoph Lengauer, Kenneth W. Kinzler, Bert Vogelstein
- **Journal**: Nature (1999) 401:616-620
- **DOI**: 10.1038/44188
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10524633/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-042` (`SFN` → `CDK1|CCNB1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Human cells with damaged DNA arrest the cell cycle in G2 in a p53-dependent manner to prevent entry into mitosis with damaged chromosomes. This study generated human colorectal cancer cell lines deficient in the p53 target gene 14-3-3 sigma (SFN) by homologous recombination and analyzed its role in maintaining the G2 checkpoint after DNA damage. In wild-type cells after DNA damage, 14-3-3 sigma binds to Cdc2 (CDK1)–cyclin B1 (CCNB1) complexes and sequesters them in the cytoplasm to maintain a stable G2 arrest, whereas in 14-3-3 sigma-deficient cells, Cdc2–cyclin B1 complexes enter the nucleus, resulting in failure to maintain G2 arrest, mitotic catastrophe, and cell death.
