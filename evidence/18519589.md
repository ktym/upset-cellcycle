# Pseudosubstrate inhibition of the anaphase-promoting complex by Acm1: regulation by proteolysis and Cdc28 phosphorylation

- **PMID**: 18519589
- **Authors**: Denis Ostapenko, Janet L. Burton, Ruiwen Wang, Mark J. Solomon
- **Journal**: Molecular and Cellular Biology (2008) 28:4971-4983
- **DOI**: 10.1128/MCB.00055-08

- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N21` (`CDC28` → `ACM1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

This study elucidates how the stability and function of Acm1, a pseudosubstrate inhibitor of APC/C-Cdh1, are regulated by phosphorylation via the cyclin-dependent kinase Cdc28 (Cdk1). Although Acm1 itself is a highly unstable protein, phosphorylation of its consensus sites by Cdc28 from late G1 through M phase promotes binding of the 14-3-3 proteins Bmh1 and Bmh2 to phosphorylated Acm1, protecting it from degradation and stabilizing it. Because inhibition of Cdc28, mutation of Cdc28 phosphorylation sites in Acm1, or deletion of BMH1/BMH2 all rapidly destabilize Acm1, Cdc28 is shown to inhibit APC/C-Cdh1 through a dual mechanism: direct phosphorylation of Cdh1 and phosphorylation-mediated stabilization of Acm1.
