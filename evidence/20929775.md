# Two histone marks establish the inner centromere and chromosome bi-orientation

- **PMID**: 20929775
- **Authors**: Yuya Yamagishi, Takashi Honda, Yuji Tanno, Yoshinori Watanabe
- **Journal**: Science (2010) 330:239-243
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.1194498
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20929775/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N20` (`HASPIN` → `BIRC5|AURKB|INCENP`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In eukaryotic mitosis, accurate localization of the chromosomal passenger complex (CPC: Aurora B, INCENP, Survivin, and Borealin) to the inner centromere between sister chromatids is essential for establishing chromosome bi-orientation. Using fission yeast and human cells, this study reveals that the inner centromere is defined by the intersection of two distinct histone phosphorylation marks. Specifically, Haspin-mediated phosphorylation of histone H3 at threonine 3 (H3-pT3) promotes nucleosome binding of the CPC subunit Survivin (BIRC5), while simultaneously Bub1-mediated phosphorylation of histone H2A recruits the CPC via Shugoshin (Sgo1/Sgo2), resulting in specific accumulation of the CPC (Aurora B) at the inner centromere where both modifications overlap.
