## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

D-type cyclins (cyclin D) integrate extracellular and intracellular mitogenic signals to activate CDK4/6, which phosphorylates and inactivates the RB tumor suppressor protein to promote the initiation of DNA replication. Through a genome-wide screen investigating the genetic basis of the response to CDK4/6 inhibition, this study identified AMBRA1 as the primary regulator responsible for cyclin D degradation. AMBRA1 functions as a substrate receptor for the Cullin 4 (CRL4) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex to mediate the ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of cyclin D. Loss of AMBRA1 leads to accumulation of cyclin D in cells and mice, promoting cell proliferation and decreasing sensitivity to CDK4/6 inhibitors.
