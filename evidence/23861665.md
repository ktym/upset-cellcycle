# Budding Yeast Greatwall and Endosulfines Control Activity and Spatial Regulation of PP2A(Cdc55) for Timely Mitotic Progression

- **PMID**: 23861665
- **Authors**: Maria Angeles Juanes, Rita Khoueiry, Thomas Kupka, Anna Castro, Ingrid Mudrak, Egon Ogris, Thierry Lorca, Simonetta Piatti
- **Journal**: PLoS Genetics (2013) 9:e1003575
- **DOI**: 10.1371/journal.pgen.1003575
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23861665/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N26` (`RIM15` → `IGO1|IGO2`), `SCE-CC-N27` (`IGO1|IGO2` → `CDC55`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In eukaryotes, Greatwall kinase phosphorylates endosulfine family proteins, enabling phosphorylated endosulfines to bind and inhibit the PP2A-B55 phosphatase to control mitotic entry. This study shows that Rim15 kinase, the budding yeast Greatwall ortholog, phosphorylates the endosulfines Igo1 and Igo2, allowing Igo1/2 to bind PP2A-Cdc55 in a cell-cycle-regulated manner. Phosphorylated Igo1 inhibits PP2A-Cdc55 activity in vitro and also contributes to nuclear export and functional regulation of PP2A-Cdc55 in yeast cells, promoting timely mitotic entry via the Cdk1 activation positive feedback loop.
