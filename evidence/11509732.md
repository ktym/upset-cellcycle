# Cohesin cleavage by separase required for anaphase and cytokinesis in human cells

- **PMID**: 11509732
- **Authors**: Sylvia Hauf, Irene C. Waizenegger, Jan-Michael Peters
- **Journal**: Science (2001) 293:1320-1323
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.1061376
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11509732/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-050` (`ESPL1` → `RAD21`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

During vertebrate cell division, the majority of cohesin dissociates from chromosome arms during prophase via a cleavage-independent pathway, whereas a small amount of cohesin remaining at centromeric regions disappears at the onset of anaphase. This study identified two specific cleavage sites (Arg172 and Arg450) in the human cohesin subunit SCC1 (RAD21) that are cleaved by the cysteine protease separase (ESPL1). Furthermore, conditional expression of non-cleavable SCC1 mutants carrying mutations at these cleavage sites in human cells completely blocked sister chromatid separation and also prevented the completion of cytokinesis, proving that separase-mediated cleavage of RAD21/SCC1 is essential for sister chromatid separation in human cells.
