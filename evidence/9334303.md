# Phosphorylation of Sic1p by G1 Cdk required for its degradation and entry into S phase

- **PMID**: 9334303
- **Authors**: R. Verma, R. S. Annan, M. J. Huddleston, S. A. Carr, G. Reynard, R. J. Deshaies
- **Journal**: Science (1997) 278:455-460
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.278.5337.455
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9334303/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-012` (`CDC28|CLN1|CLN2` → `SIC1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Transition from G1 to S phase in budding yeast requires the G1-CDK (Cln-Cdc28)-dependent degradation of Sic1p, an inhibitor of S-phase CDK (Clb5/6-Cdc28). In this study, multidimensional electrospray mass spectrometry was used to identify multiple CDK consensus sites on Sic1p (such as T5, T33, T45, and S76) phosphorylated by G1-CDK in vitro and in vivo. A Sic1p mutant lacking CDK phosphorylation sites—specifically three key sites (T5, T33, and S76)—was not ubiquitinated in a Cdc34-dependent manner in vitro, remained stable in vivo, and arrested the cell cycle at the G1/S boundary, completely blocking DNA replication initiation. Furthermore, adding purified phosphorylated Sic1p to G1 cyclin-depleted extracts allowed ubiquitination to proceed, proving that the essential function of G1-CDK is to directly phosphorylate Sic1p and target it for ubiquitination.
