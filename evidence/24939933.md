# Tension-dependent removal of pericentromeric shugoshin is an indicator of sister chromosome biorientation

- **PMID**: 24939933
- **Authors**: Olga O. Nerusheva, Stefan Galander, Josefin Fernius, David Kelly, Adele L. Marston
- **Journal**: Genes & Development (2014) 28:1291-1309
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.240291.114
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24939933/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N34` (`BUB1` → `SGO1|RTS1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The shugoshin (Sgo1) platform assembled at pericentromeric regions plays a key role in establishing sister chromosome biorientation. This study reveals that the balance of activities between kinetochore-associated Bub1 kinase (which phosphorylates histone H2A-S121) and Sgo1-bound phosphatase PP2A-Rts1 forms a tension-dependent circuit that retains Sgo1-PP2A(Rts1) at the pericentromere in the absence of tension, whereas Sgo1 reversibly dissociates from the pericentromere once tension is generated across sister kinetochores. Removal of Sgo1 triggers dissociation of condensin and Aurora B from the centromere, signaling stabilization and progression of the bioriented state.
