# Human Mps1 kinase is required for the spindle assembly checkpoint but not for centrosome duplication

- **PMID**: 11927556
- **Authors**: Volker M. Stucke, Hein H. W. Silljé, Lionel Arnaud, Erich A. Nigg
- **Journal**: EMBO J (2002) 21:1723-1732
- **DOI**: 10.1093/emboj/21.7.1723
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11927556/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-043` (`TTK` → `MAD1L1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) is a surveillance mechanism that delays the onset of anaphase until all chromosomes are properly attached to spindle microtubules. By analyzing the subcellular localization and function of human Mps1 kinase (TTK / hMps1) using antibody microinjection and siRNA knockdown, this study demonstrated that human Mps1 localizes to kinetochores during mitosis and is essential for SAC activation and mitotic arrest upon microtubule poison treatment. Furthermore, functional inhibition or depletion of Mps1 abolished the localization of the checkpoint proteins MAD1 (MAD1L1) and MAD2 to unattached kinetochores, revealing that Mps1 plays an essential upstream role in recruiting the MAD1-MAD2 complex to kinetochores and in SAC signaling.
