# EMI1 switches from being a substrate to an inhibitor of APC/CCDH1 to start the cell cycle

- **PMID**: 29875408
- **Authors**: Steven D. Cappell, Kevin G. Mark, Damien Garbett, Lindsey R. Pack, Michael Rape, Tobias Meyer
- **Journal**: Nature (2018) 558:313-317
- **DOI**: 10.1038/s41586-018-0199-7
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29875408/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-022` (`FBXO5` → `FZR1`), `HSA-CC-N07` (`FZR1` → `FBXO5`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

For mammalian cells to progress from G1 into S phase and commit to the cell cycle, they must inactivate the ubiquitin ligase complex APC/C-CDH1 (FZR1), an inactivation event that functions as the "point of no return" for cell-cycle entry. This study revealed a dual-negative feedback mechanism in which early mitotic inhibitor 1 (EMI1/FBXO5) acts both as a substrate and as an inhibitor of APC/C-CDH1. In early G1 phase, when EMI1 levels are low and APC/C-CDH1 activity is high, EMI1 is ubiquitinated and degraded as a substrate of APC/C-CDH1. However, as CDK2 activity rises and transcriptional induction causes EMI1 to accumulate, EMI1 switches to being an inhibitor of APC/C-CDH1, rapidly inactivating APC/C-CDH1 as a bistable, irreversible one-way switch to drive the transition into S and G2 phases.
