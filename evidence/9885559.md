# The phosphatase Cdc14 triggers mitotic exit by reversal of Cdk-dependent phosphorylation

- **PMID**: 9885559
- **Authors**: Visintin R, Craig K, Hwang ES, Prinz S, Tyers M, Amon A
- **Journal**: Mol Cell (1998) 2:709-718
- **DOI**: 10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80286-5
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9885559/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-049` (`CDC14` → `CDH1`), `SCE-CC-050` (`CDC14` → `SIC1|SWI5`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Inactivation of M-phase cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) is essential for eukaryotic cells to exit mitosis. This study revealed that the budding yeast protein phosphatase Cdc14 is a master regulator that triggers CDK inactivation and mitotic exit by reversing CDK-dependent phosphorylation. Specifically, Cdc14 eliminates Clb–Cdc28 activity through three coordinated pathways: dephosphorylating and activating the APC/C activator Cdh1 (Hct1) to induce APC-dependent degradation of Clb cyclins, dephosphorylating the transcription factor Swi5 to activate SIC1 transcription, and directly dephosphorylating and stabilizing the CDK inhibitor Sic1 itself.
