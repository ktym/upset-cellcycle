# Rad53-dependent phosphorylation of Swi6 and down-regulation of CLN1 and CLN2 transcription occur in response to DNA damage in Saccharomyces cerevisiae

- **PMID**: 9367985
- **Authors**: Julia M. Sidorova, Linda L. Breeden
- **Journal**: Genes & Development (1997) 11:3032-3045
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.11.22.3032
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9367985/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-019` (`RAD53` → `SWI6`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

When budding yeast experiences DNA damage (such as MMS treatment or ionizing radiation) in G1 phase, a G1 checkpoint operates to delay entry into S phase. In this study, transcription of the G1 cyclin genes CLN1 and CLN2 was shown to be transiently down-regulated in response to DNA damage in G1 phase, and both the checkpoint kinase Rad53 and Swi6, a subunit of the transcription factor SBF, were found to be required for this transcriptional repression and delay in S-phase entry. Furthermore, Swi6 undergoes RAD53-dependent phosphorylation in MMS-treated G1 cells, and Rad53 immunoprecipitated from MMS-treated cells directly phosphorylates Swi6 in vitro, proposing a checkpoint pathway in which Rad53-mediated phosphorylation of Swi6 restrains SBF activity to down-regulate CLN1/CLN2 transcription.
