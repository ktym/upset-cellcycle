# Phosphorylation by cyclin B-Cdk underlies release of mitotic exit activator Cdc14 from the nucleolus

- **PMID**: 15273393
- **Authors**: Azzam R, Chen SL, Shou W, Mah AS, Alexandru G, Nasmyth K, Annan RS, Carr SA, Deshaies RJ
- **Journal**: Science (2004) 305:516-519
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.1099402
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15273393/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-058` (`CDC28|CLB2` → `NET1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In budding yeast, the protein phosphatase Cdc14, which triggers mitotic exit, is sequestered in the nucleolus during interphase, prophase, and metaphase through binding to its inhibitor Net1 (Cfi1). Using mass spectrometry and mutational analysis, this study demonstrates that mitotic cyclin-dependent kinase (Clb-Cdk, such as Clb2-Cdc28) directly phosphorylates six consensus sites (Thr-62, Ser-166, Thr-212, Ser-252, Ser-297, and Thr-304) within the N-terminal Cdc14-binding domain of Net1, causing dissociation of the Net1–Cdc14 complex. Because non-phosphorylatable mutant cells substituting these six sites with alanine (net1-6Cdk) lose FEAR network-dependent Cdc14 release during early anaphase, phosphorylation of Net1 by Clb-Cdk is established as the direct molecular basis for releasing Cdc14 from the nucleolus.
