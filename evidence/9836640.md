## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In response to DNA damage and replication blocks, cells arrest cell cycle progression by modulating key cell cycle regulators. This study identified the checkpoint kinase Chk2 (CHEK2), the mammalian homolog of budding yeast Rad53 and fission yeast Cds1. Chk2 is rapidly phosphorylated and activated in response to DNA damage and replication blocks, with the response to DNA damage occurring in an ATM-dependent manner. Activated Chk2 phosphorylates substrates such as Cdc25C on Ser-216, linking ATM to cell cycle checkpoint regulation.
