# Chk2/hCds1 functions as a DNA damage checkpoint in G(1) by stabilizing p53

- **PMID**: 10673500
- **Authors**: N. H. Chehab, A. Malikzay, M. Appel, T. D. Halazonetis
- **Journal**: Genes & Development (2000) 14:278-288
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.14.3.278
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10673500/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-012` (`CHEK2` → `TP53`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The human DNA damage checkpoint kinase Chk2/hCds1 (CHEK2) is activated after DNA damage and induces cell cycle arrest in G1 phase. This study revealed that Chk2 directly phosphorylates p53 on Ser-20 (S20) in vitro and dissociates preformed complexes between p53 and Mdm2, which targets p53 for degradation. Furthermore, in vivo, wild-type Chk2 promoted Ser-20 phosphorylation and stabilization of p53 after DNA damage, whereas a dominant-negative mutant abrogated both, demonstrating that phosphorylation of p53 (S20) by Chk2 results in p53 stabilization and G1 checkpoint arrest.
