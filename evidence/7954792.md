# The B-type cyclin kinase inhibitor p40SIC1 controls the G1 to S transition in S. cerevisiae

- **PMID**: 7954792
- **Authors**: E. Schwob, T. Böhm, M. D. Mendenhall, K. Nasmyth
- **Journal**: Cell (1994) 79:233-244
- **DOI**: 10.1016/0092-8674(94)90193-7
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7954792/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-011` (`SIC1` → `CDC28|CLB5|CLB2`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

When budding yeast cells reach a critical size, budding, spindle pole body (SPB) duplication, and DNA replication are initiated almost simultaneously depending on Cdc28 activation by G1 cyclins (Cln1/2/3), whereas the initiation of DNA replication additionally requires Cdc28 activation by B-type cyclins (Clb1-6, especially Clb5/6). This study revealed that p40 (Sic1) is a CDK inhibitor that does not inhibit Cln-Cdc28, but specifically and potently inhibits only Clb-Cdc28 kinase complexes. Mutants of the ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme Cdc34 arrest and fail to enter S phase because they cannot degrade Sic1, whereas deleting the SIC1 gene allows DNA replication to occur even in cdc34 mutants, establishing that Clb-Cdc28 inhibition by Sic1 and its relief (degradation) are key to the G1/S transition.
