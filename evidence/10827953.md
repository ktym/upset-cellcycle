# Rapid destruction of human Cdc25A in response to DNA damage

- **PMID**: 10827953

- **Journal**: Science (2000) 288:1425-1429
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.288.5470.1425

- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-031` (`CHEK1` → `CDC25A`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

When human cells are exposed to genotoxic stresses such as ultraviolet light (UV) or ionizing radiation (IR), cell cycle progression must be rapidly halted to allow time for DNA repair. This study revealed that the phosphatase Cdc25A is rapidly degraded via the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway in response to DNA damage. This rapid destruction of Cdc25A requires phosphorylation of Cdc25A by the checkpoint kinase Chk1 (CHEK1), and loss of Cdc25A maintains inhibitory phosphorylation on cyclin E/A–Cdk2 complexes to immediately block S-phase entry and progression. This p53-independent Chk1–Cdc25A degradation pathway is crucial as an early checkpoint mechanism that prevents replication of damaged DNA.
