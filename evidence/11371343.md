# Phosphorylation of the cohesin subunit Scc1 by Polo/Cdc5 kinase regulates sister chromatid separation in yeast

- **PMID**: 11371343
- **Authors**: Alexandru G, Uhlmann F, Mechtler K, Poupart MA, Nasmyth K
- **Journal**: Cell (2001) 105:459-472
- **DOI**: 10.1016/s0092-8674(01)00362-2
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11371343/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-040` (`CDC5` → `MCD1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

At the onset of anaphase, the caspase-related protease separase (Esp1) resolves sister chromatid cohesion by cleaving the cohesin subunit Scc1 (Mcd1). This study revealed that the Polo-like kinase Cdc5 (Polo) phosphorylates serine residues (Ser175 and Ser263) adjacent to both of Scc1's separase cleavage sites, and that this phosphorylation dramatically enhances the efficiency of Scc1 cleavage by Esp1 both in vitro and in vivo. In cells lacking Pds1 (securin), *cdc5* mutation or mutation of Scc1's phosphorylation sites strongly delayed chromosome separation and Scc1 cleavage, demonstrating that Cdc5-mediated phosphorylation near Scc1's cleavage sites is an important regulatory mechanism for efficient progression of sister chromatid separation.
