# Identification of a vertebrate sister-chromatid separation inhibitor involved in transformation and tumorigenesis

- **PMID**: 10411507
- **Authors**: Hui Zou, Thomas J. McGarry, Teresita Bernal, Marc W. Kirschner
- **Journal**: Science (1999) 285:418-422
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.285.5426.418
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10411507/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-047` (`PTTG1` → `ESPL1`), `HSA-CC-048` (`CDC20` → `PTTG1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Separation of sister chromatids at the metaphase-to-anaphase transition is one of the most critical events in cell division. Based on biochemical analogy to budding yeast Pds1p and fission yeast Cut2p, this study isolated and identified a vertebrate sister-chromatid separation inhibitor, securin (vSecurin), and determined that human securin is identical to the pituitary tumor-transforming gene product (PTTG / PTTG1). Securin binds to separin (separase; ESPL1), the vertebrate homolog of yeast Esp1p/Cut1p, inhibiting its protease activity and preventing premature sister-chromatid separation. At the metaphase-to-anaphase transition, ubiquitination and degradation of securin by the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C-CDC20) liberates separase, initiating sister-chromatid separation (anaphase).
