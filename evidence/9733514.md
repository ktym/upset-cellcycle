# Enhanced phosphorylation of p53 by ATM in response to DNA damage

- **PMID**: 9733514
- **Authors**: S. Banin, L. Moyal, S. Shieh, Y. Taya, C. W. Anderson, L. Chessa, N. I. Smorodinsky, C. Prives, Y. Reiss, Y. Shiloh, Y. Ziv
- **Journal**: Science (1998) 281:1674-1677
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.281.5383.1674
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9733514/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-011` (`ATM` → `TP53`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

ATM, the product of the gene mutated in ataxia-telangiectasia, is essential for cellular responses to ionizing radiation and DNA double-strand breaks as well as G1 checkpoint control. This study demonstrated that endogenous and recombinant ATM possess protein kinase activity and specifically phosphorylate p53 in vitro on Ser-15 (S15), a site phosphorylated in vivo upon DNA damage. Furthermore, within minutes of DNA damage treatment with a radiomimetic drug, the p53-directed kinase activity of ATM is markedly enhanced without changes in ATM protein levels.
