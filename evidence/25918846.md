# TRIP13 is a protein-remodeling AAA+ ATPase that catalyzes MAD2 conformation switching

- **PMID**: 25918846
- **Authors**: Qiaozhen Ye, Scott C. Rosenberg, Arne Moeller, Jeffrey A. Speir, Tsung-Pin Su, Kevin D. Corbett
- **Journal**: eLife (2015) 4:e07367
- **DOI**: 10.7554/eLife.07367
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25918846/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N24` (`TRIP13|MAD2L1BP` → `MAD2L1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The HORMA domain protein MAD2 drives the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) by undergoing a conformational switch from an inactive "open" form (O-MAD2) to a signaling-active "closed" form (C-MAD2) to assemble the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC). Through structural and biochemical analyses, this study proves that the AAA+ ATPase TRIP13 is a protein-remodeling enzyme that specifically recognizes C-MAD2 via the adapter protein p31comet (MAD2L1BP) and uses the energy of ATP hydrolysis to partially unfold C-MAD2 and catalyze its conformational conversion back to O-MAD2. This conversion of C-MAD2 to O-MAD2 by TRIP13 and p31comet disassembles the MCC and silences the SAC.
