# Anaphase initiation in Saccharomyces cerevisiae is controlled by the APC-dependent degradation of the anaphase inhibitor Pds1p

- **PMID**: 8985178
- **Authors**: Cohen-Fix O, Peters JM, Kirschner MW, Koshland D
- **Journal**: Genes Dev (1996) 10:3081-3093
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.10.24.3081
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8985178/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-037` (`CDC20|CDC16|CDC23|CDC27` → `PDS1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Initiation of anaphase had been postulated to be controlled by ubiquitin-dependent proteolysis of an unknown inhibitor. In this study, the budding yeast Pds1 protein (securin) was demonstrated to be an anaphase inhibitor that is degraded in an anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C)-dependent manner. Pds1p is degraded at the time of anaphase initiation, expression of nondegradable Pds1p variants inhibits anaphase onset, and Pds1p is directly ubiquitinated by Xenopus APC. This established that APC/C-dependent ubiquitination and degradation of Pds1p is the trigger for anaphase initiation.
