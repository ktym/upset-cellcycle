## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Cyclin-dependent kinases CDK4 and CDK6 form complexes with D-type cyclins and phosphorylate the retinoblastoma tumor suppressor protein RB to drive cell proliferation. Because RB can also be phosphorylated by other cyclin–CDK complexes, understanding how cyclin D–CDK4/6 specifically recognizes RB to drive G1 progression was an important question. This study revealed that cyclin D–CDK4/6 achieves substrate recognition by specifically docking onto one side of an alpha-helix located in the C-terminal region of RB. This C-terminal helix of RB is not recognized by cyclins E, A, or B, and mutating this helix prevents RB phosphorylation, induces G1 arrest, and enhances the tumor-suppressive function of RB.
