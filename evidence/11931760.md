# The dissociation of cohesin from chromosomes in prophase is regulated by Polo-like kinase

- **PMID**: 11931760
- **Authors**: Izabela Sumara, Elisabeth Vorlaufer, P. Todd Stukenberg, Olaf Kelm, Norbert Redemann, Erich A. Nigg, Jan-Michael Peters
- **Journal**: Mol Cell (2002) 9:515-525
- **DOI**: 10.1016/s1097-2765(02)00473-2
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11931760/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-038` (`PLK1` → `STAG2`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

During vertebrate mitosis, the bulk of the cohesin complex holding sister chromatids together dissociates from chromosome arms in a cleavage-independent manner from prophase to prometaphase (the prophase pathway), without waiting for proteolytic cleavage by separase at the onset of anaphase. Using *Xenopus* egg extracts and cohesin derived from human cells, this study revealed that Polo-like kinase (Plx1 / PLK1) is necessary and sufficient for the dissociation of cohesin from chromosomes during prophase. PLK1 directly phosphorylates subunits of the cohesin complex (particularly SA2 / STAG2 and Scc1) and reduces its binding affinity for chromatin, thereby driving the release of cohesin from chromosome arms during prophase.
