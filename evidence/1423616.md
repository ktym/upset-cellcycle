# A mammalian cell cycle checkpoint pathway utilizing p53 and GADD45 is defective in ataxia-telangiectasia

- **PMID**: 1423616
- **Authors**: Michael B. Kastan, Qimin Zhan, Wafik S. el-Deiry, France Carrier, Tyler Jacks, William V. Walsh, Beverly S. Plunkett, Bert Vogelstein, Albert J. Fornace Jr.
- **Journal**: Cell (1992) 71:587-597
- **DOI**: 10.1016/0092-8674(92)90593-2
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1423616/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-041` (`TP53` → `SFN|GADD45A`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Cell cycle checkpoints following DNA damage are critically important for enhancing cell survival and limiting the accumulation of mutations. This study revealed that transcriptional induction of the human GADD45 (GADD45A) gene by ionizing radiation (IR) strictly depends on wild-type p53 (TP53) function, and that both p53 accumulation and GADD45 induction are defective in cells derived from ataxia-telangiectasia (AT) patients. Furthermore, it was demonstrated that wild-type p53 protein, but not mutant p53, binds strongly to an evolutionarily conserved cis-element within the GADD45 gene to directly induce its transcription. These findings established a cell cycle checkpoint pathway in which p53 directly induces GADD45 transcription in response to DNA damage.
