# The cortical protein Lte1 promotes mitotic exit by inhibiting the spindle position checkpoint kinase Kin4

- **PMID**: 21670215
- **Authors**: Daniela Trinca Bertazzi, Bahtiyar Kurtulmus, Gislene Pereira
- **Journal**: Journal of Cell Biology (2011) 193:1033-1048
- **DOI**: 10.1083/jcb.201101056
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21670215/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N38` (`LTE1` → `KIN4`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In the spindle position checkpoint (SPOC), the Kin4 kinase and the Bub2-Bfa1 GAP complex inhibit the mitotic exit-promoting GTPase Tem1. During a normal cell cycle, Kin4 localizes to the mother-bound SPB (mSPB) while Bub2-Bfa1 localizes to the daughter-bound SPB (dSPB), but the mechanism governing this spatial asymmetry remained unclear. This study reveals that the daughter-cell (bud) cortical protein Lte1 physically interacts with catalytically active Kin4, inhibits its kinase activity, and promotes Kin4 hyperphosphorylation, thereby restricting Kin4 binding to the mSPB and excluding Kin4 from the dSPB to allow Tem1 activation and mitotic exit.
