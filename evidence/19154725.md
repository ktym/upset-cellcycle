# Spindle alignment regulates the dynamic association of checkpoint proteins with yeast spindle pole bodies

- **PMID**: 19154725
- **Authors**: Ayse Koca Caydasi, Gislene Pereira
- **Journal**: Developmental Cell (2009) 16:146-156
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.devcel.2008.10.013
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19154725/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N37` (`KIN4` → `BFA1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The spindle position checkpoint (SPOC) in budding yeast delays mitotic exit when the anaphase spindle fails to elongate properly along the mother–daughter axis. Using fluorescence recovery after photobleaching (FRAP) and related approaches, this study reveals that upon SPOC activation triggered by spindle misalignment, the binding dynamics (turnover) of the Bub2–Bfa1 complex at spindle pole bodies (SPBs) change dramatically via phosphorylation of Bfa1 by the Kin4 kinase. Phosphorylation of Bfa1 by Kin4 destabilizes Bfa1 residence at the SPB, sequestering it away from the Cdc5 kinase and preventing inhibitory phosphorylation by Cdc5 to maintain Tem1 inhibition.
