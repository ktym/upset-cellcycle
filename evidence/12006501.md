# Crystal structure of the tetrameric Mad1-Mad2 core complex: implications of a 'safety belt' binding mechanism for the spindle checkpoint

- **PMID**: 12006501
- **Authors**: Lucia Sironi, Marina Mapelli, Stefan Knapp, Anna De Antoni, Kuan-Teh Jeang, Andrea Musacchio
- **Journal**: EMBO J (2002) 21:2496-2506
- **DOI**: 10.1093/emboj/21.10.2496
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12006501/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-044` (`MAD1L1` → `MAD2L1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC), Mad2 (MAD2L1) is recruited to unattached kinetochores and subsequently sequesters the APC/C activator Cdc20 to inhibit anaphase onset. Mad1 (MAD1L1) binds directly to Mad2 and plays an essential role in localizing Mad2 to kinetochores. This study determined the X-ray crystal structure of the human Mad1-Mad2 core complex at 2.05 Å resolution, revealing a 2:2 heterotetrameric structure in which two molecules of Mad2 bind to two molecules of Mad1 forming a parallel coiled-coil homodimer. Structural analysis uncovered a "safety belt" mechanism in which the C-terminal region of Mad2 undergoes a major conformational change to wrap around and lock the Mad1 binding peptide motif in place, elucidating the structural basis by which Mad1 tightly binds and tethers Mad2 at kinetochores and regulates its handoff to Cdc20.
