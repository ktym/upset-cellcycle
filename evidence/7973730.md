# Cell cycle control by a complex of the cyclin HCS26 (PCL1) and the kinase PHO85

- **PMID**: 7973730
- **Authors**: F. Hernán Espinoza, Joseph Ogas, Ira Herskowitz, David O. Morgan
- **Journal**: Science (1994) 266:1388-1391
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.7973730
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7973730/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-017` (`PCL1` → `PHO85`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Although G1 progression in budding yeast is primarily driven by complexes of the cyclins Cln1/2/3 and the Cdc28 kinase, this study uncovered a G1 control pathway mediated by a second cyclin-CDK complex. The G1 cyclin-like protein Hcs26 (Pcl1: Pho85 cyclin 1), previously identified as a target of SBF (Swi4-Swi6), does not bind Cdc28 but specifically associates with the protein kinase Pho85, which shares high homology with Cdc28, to induce kinase activity. Hcs26-Pho85 kinase activity rises periodically during G1 phase, and PHO85 is essential for G1 progression and viability in cells lacking the major G1 cyclins CLN1 and CLN2, demonstrating that the Pcl1-Pho85 complex cooperates with Cln-Cdc28 to control the G1/S transition.
