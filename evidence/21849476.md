# A phosphatase threshold sets the level of Cdk1 activity in early mitosis in budding yeast

- **PMID**: 21849476
- **Authors**: Stacy L. Harvey, Germán Enciso, Noah Dephoure, Steven P. Gygi, Jeremy Gunawardena, Douglas R. Kellogg
- **Journal**: Molecular Biology of the Cell (2011) 22:3595-3608
- **DOI**: 10.1091/mbc.E11-04-0340

- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N24` (`CDC55` → `SWE1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

During entry into mitosis in budding yeast, Cdk1 activity rises in a stepwise manner despite the gradual synthesis of cyclins. Swe1 (the Wee1 homolog), a major inhibitory kinase of Cdk1, engages in mutual regulation with Cdk1: initial phosphorylation by Cdk1 activates Swe1 to inhibit Cdk1, whereas further phosphorylation inactivates Swe1. This study reveals that protein phosphatase 2A containing the Cdc55 regulatory subunit (PP2A-Cdc55) opposes the initial phosphorylation of Swe1 by Cdk1 and sets a threshold for Swe1 activation. This allows a low, constant level of Cdk1 activity to escape Swe1-mediated inhibition in early mitosis and drive early mitotic events.
