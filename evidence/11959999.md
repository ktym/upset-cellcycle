# Bfa1 can regulate Tem1 function independently of Bub2 in the mitotic exit network of Saccharomyces cerevisiae

- **PMID**: 11959999
- **Authors**: Ro HS, Song S, Lee KS
- **Journal**: Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (2002) 99:5436-5441
- **DOI**: 10.1073/pnas.062059999
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11959999/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-055` (`BUB2|BFA1` → `TEM1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In the budding yeast mitotic exit network (MEN), the Ras-like GTPase Tem1 acts as an upstream activating switch and is negatively regulated by Bub2 and Bfa1, which colocalize at the spindle pole body (SPB). While Bub2 and Bfa1 form a complex that functions as a two-component GAP (GTPase-activating protein) for Tem1, this study revealed that Bfa1 can also inhibit Tem1 independently of Bub2. Overexpression of *BFA1* induced a potent telophase arrest even in a *bub2* deletion strain, and Bfa1 was shown to directly bind Tem1 and inhibit its interaction with the downstream effector Cdc15. Thus, Bub2–Bfa1 dually suppresses Tem1 function not only by inactivating Tem1 as a two-component GAP, but also through Bfa1 alone inhibiting the association between Tem1 and Cdc15.
