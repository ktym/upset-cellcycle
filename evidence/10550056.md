# Control of the DNA damage checkpoint by chk1 and rad53 protein kinases through distinct mechanisms

- **PMID**: 10550056
- **Authors**: Sanchez Y, Bachant J, Wang H, Hu F, Liu D, Tetzlaff M, Elledge SJ
- **Journal**: Science (1999) 286:1166-1171
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.286.5442.1166
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10550056/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-042` (`CHK1` → `PDS1`), `SCE-CC-043` (`RAD53` → `CDC5`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In response to DNA damage, cells activate checkpoint pathways to arrest cell cycle progression. This study revealed that in budding yeast, the protein kinases Chk1 and Rad53 control the DNA damage checkpoint in parallel through distinct mechanisms. In response to DNA damage, Chk1 phosphorylates and stabilizes Pds1 (securin), maintaining Pds1 abundance to prevent anaphase entry. Meanwhile, Rad53 acts through regulation of the Polo-like kinase Cdc5 to maintain active Cdk1 (Cdc28) and prevent anaphase entry and mitotic exit, demonstrating that the G2/M DNA damage checkpoint is executed through two parallel branches: the Chk1–Pds1 pathway and the Rad53–Cdc5 pathway.
