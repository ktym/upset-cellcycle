# The cell-cycle regulatory protein Cks1 is required for SCF(Skp2)-mediated ubiquitinylation of p27

- **PMID**: 11231585
- **Authors**: D. Ganoth, G. Bornstein, T. K. Ko, B. Larsen, M. Tyers, M. Pagano, A. Hershko
- **Journal**: Nature Cell Biology (2001) 3:321-324
- **DOI**: 10.1038/35060126
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11231585/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-021` (`SKP2|SKP1|CUL1|CKS1B` → `CDKN1B`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Degradation of the cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) inhibitor p27 (CDKN1B) by the ubiquitin-proteasome system is essential for the G1/S transition and is mediated by the SCF(Skp2) complex (Skp1-Cul1-F-box protein Skp2), yet p27 ubiquitylation could not be reconstituted with purified proteins alone. Through biochemical fractionation, this study identified the cell-cycle regulatory protein Cks1 (CKS1B) as an essential cofactor for SCF(Skp2)-mediated ubiquitylation of p27. Cks1 directly binds to Skp2 and dramatically enhances the binding of Thr-187-phosphorylated p27 to Skp2, thereby enabling p27 ubiquitylation and degradation.
