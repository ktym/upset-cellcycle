# Control of cyclin ubiquitination by CDK-regulated binding of Hct1 to the anaphase promoting complex

- **PMID**: 9831566
- **Authors**: Zachariae W, Schwab M, Nasmyth K, Seufert W
- **Journal**: Science (1998) 282:1721-1724
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.282.5394.1721
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9831566/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-048` (`CDC28|CLB2|CLB5` → `CDH1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Ubiquitination and degradation of M-phase cyclins (Clb) by the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C) and its substrate-specific activator Hct1 (Cdh1) are essential for cells to exit mitosis and maintain the G1 phase. This study revealed that cyclin-dependent kinases (Cdc28–Clb complexes) directly phosphorylate Hct1, and that this phosphorylation blocks the physical association between Hct1 and the APC/C. A non-phosphorylatable mutant of Hct1 in which CDK phosphorylation sites were substituted with alanines was refractory to inhibition by Cdc28, constitutively bound to the APC, and induced Clb2 degradation. Through this mechanism, highly active Clb–Cdc28 from S phase to M phase inhibits its own inactivator APC–Hct1, whereas the decline in CDK activity (and dephosphorylation) at the end of mitosis allows Hct1 to bind the APC and complete cyclin degradation, forming a bistable switch.
