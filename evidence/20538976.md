# Loss of human Greatwall results in G2 arrest and multiple mitotic defects due to deregulation of the cyclin B-Cdc2/PP2A balance

- **PMID**: 20538976
- **Authors**: Andrew Burgess, Suzanne Vigneron, Estelle Brioudes, Jean-Claude Labbé, Thierry Lorca, Anna Castro
- **Journal**: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America (2010) 107:12564-12569
- **DOI**: 10.1073/pnas.0914191107
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20538976/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N18` (`MASTL` → `ENSA|ARPP19`), `HSA-CC-N19` (`ENSA|ARPP19` → `PPP2R2A`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Entry into and maintenance of mitosis require not only the activation of cyclin B-Cdc2 (CDK1), but also the inhibition of protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A), which dephosphorylates CDK1 substrates. This study demonstrates that human MASTL (microtubule-associated serine/threonine kinase-like) is the functional ortholog of the Xenopus and Drosophila Greatwall kinase. Complete depletion of MASTL (Greatwall) in human cells causes G2 arrest, while partial depletion leads to multiple mitotic defects including abnormal chromosome condensation, spindle checkpoint failure, and cytokinesis defects, all of which are rescued by simultaneous knockdown or inhibition of PP2A. Thus, MASTL (Greatwall) inhibits PP2A (PP2A-B55) via downstream effectors (ENSA/ARPP19) to maintain the phosphorylation balance of cyclin B-CDK1 substrates, ensuring mitotic entry and progression in human cells.
