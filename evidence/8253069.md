# Properties of Saccharomyces cerevisiae wee1 and its differential regulation of p34CDC28 in response to G1 and G2 cyclins

- **Authors**: Robert N. Booher, Raymond J. Deshaies, Marc W. Kirschner
- **Journal**: The EMBO Journal (1993) 12:3417-3426
- **DOI**: 10.1002/j.1460-2075.1993.tb06016.x
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8253069/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-030` (`SWE1` → `CDC28`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In fission yeast, the Wee1 protein kinase phosphorylates the Tyr-15 residue of Cdc2 to negatively regulate entry into M phase. This study cloned and identified SWE1 (Saccharomyces wee1) as the gene encoding the Wee1 homolog in budding yeast. Overexpression of SWE1 causes cell cycle arrest in G2 phase with short spindles, whereas deletion of swe1 abolishes the G2 delay observed in mutants lacking the tyrosine phosphatase mih1 (the cdc25 homolog). Furthermore, biochemical analyses revealed a cyclin-specific regulatory mechanism whereby the Swe1 kinase directly phosphorylates the Tyr-19 residue of p34CDC28 (Cdc28) complexed with the G2 cyclin Clb2 to inactivate it, but neither phosphorylates nor inactivates Cdc28 complexed with the G1 cyclin Cln2.
