# Cascades of multisite phosphorylation control Sic1 destruction at the onset of S phase

- **PMID**: 21993622
- **Authors**: Mardo Kõivomägi, Ervin Valk, Rainis Venta, Anna Iofik, Martin Lepiku, Eva Rose M. Balog, Seth M. Rubin, David O. Morgan, Mart Loog
- **Journal**: Nature (2011) 480:128-131
- **DOI**: 10.1038/nature10560
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21993622/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N11` (`CKS1` → `CDC28`), `SCE-CC-N12` (`CDC28|CLB5` → `SIC1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

This study elucidates the molecular mechanism by which the S-phase CDK inhibitor Sic1 undergoes processive multisite phosphorylation cascades leading to SCF(Cdc4)-dependent degradation at the G1/S transition in budding yeast. Destruction of Sic1 involves not only Cln2-Cdk1 (Cdc28) but also Clb5-Cdk1: cyclin-specific docking motifs (LP and RxL motifs) and Cks1, the phospho-adaptor subunit of Cdk1, bind to primed phosphorylated sites to drive processive N-to-C-terminal multisite phosphorylation. In particular, Clb5-Cdk1 phosphorylates its own inhibitor Sic1 via RxL docking and Cks1 to complete phosphodegron formation, generating positive feedback that produces switch-like Sic1 destruction and S-phase entry.
