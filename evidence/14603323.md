- **PMID**: 14603323
- **Authors**: Luca Busino, Maddalena Donzelli, Massimo Chiesa, Daniele Guardavaccaro, Dvora Ganoth, N. Valerio Dorrello, Avram Hershko, Michele Pagano, Giulio F. Draetta
- **Journal**: Nature (2003) 426:87-91
- **DOI**: 10.1038/nature02082
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14603323/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-031` (`CHEK1` → `CDC25A`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The Cdc25A phosphatase is an essential factor that dephosphorylates cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) to promote cell-cycle progression, but upon DNA damage or replication fork stalling it is hyperphosphorylated by the ATM/ATR-activated checkpoint kinases Chk1 (CHEK1) and Chk2 and undergoes ubiquitin-dependent degradation. This study identified beta-TrCP as the F-box protein that recognizes phosphorylated Cdc25A and targets it for degradation by the Skp1/Cul1/F-box (SCF) ubiquitin ligase complex. Suppression of beta-TrCP1 and beta-TrCP2 expression by siRNA caused hyperaccumulation of Cdc25A in cells progressing through S phase and inhibited ionizing radiation-induced Cdc25A degradation, resulting in radioresistant DNA synthesis (an intra-S-phase checkpoint defect). This established the molecular mechanism by which Chk1-mediated phosphorylation of Cdc25A triggers SCF-betaTrCP-mediated ubiquitination and degradation.
