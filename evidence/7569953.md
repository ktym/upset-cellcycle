# Myt1: a membrane-associated inhibitory kinase that phosphorylates Cdc2 on both threonine-14 and tyrosine-15

- **PMID**: 7569953

- **Journal**: Science (1995) 270:86-90
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.270.5233.86

- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-034` (`PKMYT1` → `CDK1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Cdc2 (CDK1) is the cyclin-dependent kinase that controls entry into mitosis during the cell cycle, and its enzymatic activity is negatively regulated by phosphorylation on threonine 14 (Thr14 / T14) and tyrosine 15 (Tyr15 / Y15). In contrast to Wee1, which is localized in the nucleus and phosphorylates only Tyr15, the identity of the enzyme responsible for phosphorylating Thr14 remained unknown for a long time. This study isolated and cloned Myt1 (PKMYT1), a novel membrane-associated inhibitory kinase belonging to the Wee1 family, from Xenopus egg extracts and revealed that Myt1 directly phosphorylates both Thr14 and Tyr15 residues of Cdc2 to potently inhibit Cdc2 activity.
