# MPS1/Mph1 phosphorylates the kinetochore protein KNL1/Spc7 to recruit SAC components

- **PMID**: 22660415
- **Authors**: Yuya Yamagishi, Ching-Hui Yang, Yuji Tanno, Yoshinori Watanabe
- **Journal**: Nature Cell Biology (2012) 14:746-752
- **DOI**: 10.1038/ncb2515
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22660415/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-N21` (`TTK` → `KNL1`), `HSA-CC-N22` (`KNL1` → `BUB1|BUB3`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) monitors attachments between kinetochores and spindle microtubules, delaying anaphase onset until all chromosomes achieve bioriented alignment to ensure accurate chromosome segregation. Using fission yeast and human cells, this study reveals that the evolutionarily conserved kinase MPS1 (TTK; Mph1 in fission yeast) directly phosphorylates conserved MELT-like repeat sequences in the kinetochore scaffold protein KNL1 (Spc7 in fission yeast). These phosphorylated MELT sequences serve as docking sites to recruit the BUB1-BUB3 complex to unattached kinetochores, triggering the accumulation of downstream SAC components (such as Mad1-Mad2) and accurate chromosome alignment.
