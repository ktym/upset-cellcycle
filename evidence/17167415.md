# CDK-dependent phosphorylation of Sld2 and Sld3 initiates DNA replication in budding yeast

- **PMID**: 17167415
- **Authors**: Seiji Tanaka, Toshiko Umemori, Kazuyuki Hirai, Sachiko Muramatsu, Yoichiro Kamimura, Hiroyuki Araki
- **Journal**: Nature (2007) 445:328-332
- **DOI**: 10.1038/nature05465
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17167415/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-025` (`CDC28|CLB5` → `SLD2|SLD3`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Initiation of chromosomal DNA replication at the onset of S phase in eukaryotes requires the activity of cyclin-dependent kinase (S-CDK: Clb5/6-Cdc28), but its minimal essential substrates had long remained unknown. This study identifies Sld3, in addition to the replication factor Sld2, as an essential target of CDK, and demonstrates that CDK-dependent phosphorylation of Sld3 (on Thr-600 and Ser-622) creates a binding site for the N-terminal BRCT pair of the BRCT domain-containing protein Dpb11. Meanwhile, CDK-phosphorylated Sld2 binds to the C-terminal BRCT pair of Dpb11. Because combining a phosphomimetic mutant of Sld2 (Sld2-11D) with a mutation bypassing the Sld3-Dpb11 interaction allowed CDK-independent DNA replication to proceed, phosphorylation of Sld2 and Sld3 by S-CDK and their binding to Dpb11 are established as the minimal essential CDK-regulated steps driving DNA replication initiation.
