# Plk1 promotes nuclear translocation of human Cdc25C during prophase

- **PMID**: 11897663
- **Authors**: Fumiko Toyoshima-Morimoto, Eri Taniguchi, Eisuke Nishida
- **Journal**: EMBO Rep (2002) 3:341-348
- **DOI**: 10.1093/embo-reports/kvf069
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11897663/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-036` (`PLK1` → `CDC25C`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In vertebrate cells, nuclear accumulation of active MPF (CDK1-cyclin B1) during prophase is essential for coordinating mitotic events, and the MPF-activating phosphatase Cdc25C also translocates into the nucleus during prophase. This study revealed that Polo-like kinase 1 (Plk1) directly phosphorylates the Ser198 residue located within the nuclear export signal (NES) of human Cdc25C, thereby promoting its nuclear translocation. A non-phosphorylatable Cdc25C mutant with Ser198 substituted to alanine lost nuclear accumulation during prophase, whereas a phosphomimetic (aspartate-substituted) mutant was inhibited from nuclear export and accumulated in the nucleus. This elucidated the molecular mechanism by which PLK1-mediated phosphorylation of CDC25C drives nuclear translocation of CDC25C during the G2/M transition.
