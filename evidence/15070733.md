# M-phase kinases induce phospho-dependent ubiquitination of somatic Wee1 by SCFbeta-TrCP

- **PMID**: 15070733

- **Journal**: Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (2004) 101:4419-4424
- **DOI**: 10.1073/pnas.0307700101
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15070733/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-037` (`PLK1` → `WEE1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

Somatic Wee1 (Wee1A) kinase phosphorylates Tyr15 of Cdc2 (CDK1) to suppress premature mitotic entry during G2 phase, but is rapidly degraded at the onset of M phase to allow full activation of CDK1. This study revealed that human somatic Wee1 is ubiquitinated in a phosphorylation-dependent manner by the E3 ubiquitin ligase complex SCF-beta-TrCP and targeted for proteasomal degradation. Furthermore, it demonstrated that the M-phase kinases Polo-like kinase 1 (Plk1) and Cdc2 (CDK1) phosphorylate Ser53 and Ser123 of Wee1, respectively, to generate a beta-TrCP binding motif (phosphodegron), cooperatively inducing recognition, ubiquitination, and degradation of Wee1 by SCF-beta-TrCP.
