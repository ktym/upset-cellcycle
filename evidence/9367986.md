# Phosphorylation- and ubiquitin-dependent degradation of the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor Far1p in budding yeast

- **PMID**: 9367986
- **Authors**: S. Henchoz, Y. Chi, B. Catarin, I. Herskowitz, R. J. Deshaies, M. Peter
- **Journal**: Genes & Development (1997) 11:3046-3060
- **DOI**: 10.1101/gad.11.22.3046
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9367986/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-010` (`CDC28|CLN2` → `FAR1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The budding yeast CDK inhibitor Far1p induces G1 arrest in response to mating pheromone (alpha-factor), and its stability is tightly regulated throughout the cell cycle such that wild-type Far1p is stable only during G1 phase. In this study, genetic and biochemical analyses proved that degradation of Far1p at the G1/S transition is mediated by the ubiquitin-proteasome system and requires the E2 enzyme Cdc34p and the SCF ubiquitin ligase complex (Cdc4p, Cdc53p, and Skp1p). Furthermore, an in vitro reconstitution system and analysis of a degradation-resistant mutant (far1-22: S87P) revealed that phosphorylation of Far1p by the Cdc28p-Cln2p kinase complex (particularly at the site containing Ser-87) directly triggers its recognition and ubiquitination by the SCF-Cdc4 complex.
