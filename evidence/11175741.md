# Repression of origin assembly in metaphase depends on inhibition of RLF-B/Cdt1 by geminin

- **PMID**: 11175741
- **Authors**: Shusuke Tada, Anatoliy Li, Domenico Maiorano, Marcel Méchali, J. Julian Blow
- **Journal**: Nat Cell Biol (2001) 3:107-113
- **DOI**: 10.1038/35055000
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11175741/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-028` (`GMNN` → `CDT1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

For eukaryotic chromosomal DNA to be accurately replicated once per cell cycle, the licensing reaction that loads the Mcm2-7 complex onto replication origins must be strictly repressed during the latter half of the cell cycle (from S phase to metaphase). In this study, the replication licensing factor RLF-B was purified from *Xenopus* egg extracts and shown to be identical to Cdt1 (CDT1), and it was demonstrated that RLF-B/Cdt1 is directly bound and inhibited by Geminin (GMNN). Immunodepletion of Geminin from metaphase extracts enabled the assembly of licensed origins on chromatin, and stimulation of origin assembly by CDK inhibition occurred only after Geminin removal. Thus, binding inhibition of Cdt1 by Geminin plays a major role in preventing the re-licensing of replication origins during mitosis.
