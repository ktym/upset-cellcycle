# Premature Silencing of the Spindle Assembly Checkpoint Is Prevented by the Bub1-H2A-Sgo1-PP2A Axis in Saccharomyces cerevisiae

- **PMID**: 28040741
- **Authors**: Fengzhi Jin, Michael Bokros, Yanchang Wang
- **Journal**: Genetics (2017) 205:1169-1178
- **DOI**: 10.1534/genetics.116.195727
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28040741/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N34` (`BUB1` → `SGO1|RTS1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) prevents anaphase onset until all chromosomes achieve bipolar attachment and generate tension at kinetochores. This study showed in budding yeast that mutants lacking the Bub1 kinase domain or carrying a mutation at the Bub1 phosphorylation site on histone H2A (S121) are competent for initial SAC activation, but fail to maintain a prolonged checkpoint arrest in the presence of tensionless attachments, leading to premature SAC silencing. Furthermore, mutations in the H2A-binding motif or PP2A-Rts1-binding motif of Sgo1 similarly cause anaphase entry in the absence of tension, demonstrating that the Bub1–H2A–Sgo1–PP2A(Rts1) axis prevents premature SAC silencing until chromosome bipolar attachment is established.
