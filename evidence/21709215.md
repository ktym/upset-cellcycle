# Lte1 promotes mitotic exit by controlling the localization of the spindle position checkpoint kinase Kin4

- **PMID**: 21709215
- **Authors**: Jill E. Falk, Leon Y. Chan, Angelika Amon
- **Journal**: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America (2011) 108:12584-12590
- **DOI**: 10.1073/pnas.1107784108
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21709215/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-N38` (`LTE1` → `KIN4`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The budding yeast mitotic exit network (MEN) is activated by the Tem1 GTPase and inhibited by the mother-cell-localized SPOC kinase Kin4. Although bud-localized Lte1 was long thought to act as a GEF for Tem1, this study demonstrates that Lte1's primary function is to control the subcellular localization of Kin4. Lte1 acts on Kin4 that leaks into the bud to prevent Kin4 from associating with the daughter-bound SPB (dSPB), thereby allowing Tem1 activation and mitotic exit progression when the dSPB of a properly aligned spindle enters the bud.
