# Dissociation of cohesin from chromosome arms and loss of arm cohesion during early mitosis depends on phosphorylation of SA2

- **PMID**: 15737063
- **Authors**: Sylvia Hauf, Eva Roitinger, Birgit Koch, Christina M. Dittrich, Karl Mechtler, Jan-Michael Peters
- **Journal**: PLoS Biol (2005) 3:e69
- **DOI**: 10.1371/journal.pbio.0030069
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15737063/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `HSA-CC-038` (`PLK1` → `STAG2`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

In vertebrate mitosis, the bulk of cohesin dissociates from chromosome arms during prophase and prometaphase without cleavage of Scc1 (RAD21), a process that requires Polo-like kinase 1 (Plk1). In this study, mitosis-specific phosphorylation sites on human cohesin subunits were comprehensively identified by mass spectrometry, and human cell lines expressing non-phosphorylatable mutants of Scc1 and SA2 (STAG2) at physiological levels were generated for functional analysis. The results directly prove that while Scc1 phosphorylation is dispensable for prophase cohesin dissociation, phosphorylation of the C-terminal region of SA2 (STAG2) by Plk1 is essential for cohesin dissociation from chromosome arms and the resolution of arm cohesion.
