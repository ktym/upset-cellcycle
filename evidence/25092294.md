## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

When the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) is activated, the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC)—composed of Mad2, BubR1, Bub3, and Cdc20—is formed to inhibit the APC/C and halt progression into anaphase until chromosomes are properly aligned. Once all chromosomes achieve bioriented attachment to spindle microtubules, the SAC must be rapidly silenced, a process known to require the Mad2-binding protein p31comet (MAD2L1BP) and ATP hydrolysis. Through biochemical fractionation of HeLa cell extracts, this study identified the AAA-ATPase TRIP13 as the enzyme that acts jointly with p31comet and ATP to disassemble the MCC. TRIP13 collaborates with p31comet in an ATP-hydrolysis-dependent manner to release closed Mad2 from the MCC, disassembling the MCC and relieving SAC-mediated APC/C inhibition.
