# The transcription factor Swi5 regulates expression of the cyclin kinase inhibitor p40SIC1

- **PMID**: 8816483
- **Authors**: Knapp D, Bhoite L, Stillman DJ, Nasmyth K
- **Journal**: Mol Cell Biol (1996) 16:5701-5707
- **DOI**: 10.1128/MCB.16.10.5701
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8816483/
- **Related Interaction IDs**: `SCE-CC-051` (`SWI5` → `SIC1`)

## Summary of Cell-Cycle Regulation

The budding yeast CDK inhibitor p40SIC1 (Sic1) is degraded and disappears at the G1/S transition, and re-accumulates from late M phase after nuclear division into G1 phase. In this study, the transcription factor SWI5 was identified from a screen for suppressor mutations allowing DNA replication in the absence of Cdc4 function, revealing that high SIC1 mRNA accumulation in late M phase depends on Swi5. Swi5 localizes to the cytoplasm throughout S, G2, and M phases, but enters the nucleus in late anaphase. This cell-cycle-regulated nuclear translocation of Swi5 triggers a transient burst of SIC1 transcription at the end of anaphase, contributing to the inactivation of Clb/Cdc28 kinase and subsequent maintenance of G1 phase.
